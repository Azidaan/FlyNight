# D10 Пересвет, блики фар и HDR: адаптация фоторецептора и тон-маппинг ради детекции

↑ [[07 Исследования/Детектор с мухой/Детектор с мухой|Детектор с мухой]]

> Детекция при пересвете и бликах фар, HDR и RAW-адаптеры для детекторов (RAW-Adapter и др.), обучаемый тон-маппинг ради детекции, биоинспирированная адаптация фоторецептора для HDR; приросты; что из этого делает фоторецептор и ламина мухи.

## Суть
The strongest evidence for HDR/glare robustness comes from moving the tone curve inside the detector's training loop: a small learnable ISP or exposure/fusion module placed before (or inside) the backbone and optimised by the detection loss (RAW-Adapter, RAOD, RAM, Neural Auto-Exposure, Neural Exposure Fusion). These gains are measured on RAW/HDR sensor data (20-24 bit), where information above the 8-bit clip still exists. On 8-bit sRGB video such as BDD100K, the transferable pieces are much smaller: illumination-invariant input features (YOLA: log domain + zero-sum kernels, 0.008M params), glare/flare augmentation (NightAug in 2PCNet), and glare-aware confidence recalibration (Ma & Aelterman, on BDD100K). Biologically, this is what the photoreceptor (Weber/Naka-Rushton gain control) and the lamina L1/L2 (centre-surround contrast, histogram-equalising coding) do. The project's existing photoreceptor+lamina front-end (tracking/retina.py) is already exactly such a fixed bio-tone-mapper; the open question is only whether feeding it to YOLO26 as an input channel helps a detector whose JPEG input has already clipped the headlight cores.

## Место мухи
Front-end / input adapter, not a box proposer. Concretely: (a) Full-frame bio-ISP channels: run the project's Retina (R1-R6 photoreceptor: Naka-Rushton I/(I+I_adapt) with half-saturation tracking the local mean over tau_adapt ~0.3 s at 30 Hz; lamina L1/L2: centre minus surround divided by local spread) on every frame of the 30 Hz video, and feed photoreceptor and lamina maps as 1-2 extra input channels to YOLO26s next to RGB (first conv widened, new weights zero-initialised). This is the RAW-Adapter 'input-level adapter' and YOLA 'illumination-invariant module' idea, but with fly physiology and, unlike any static ISP, a temporal adaptation state carried across 30 Hz frames: a pixel that was dark before a headlight sweeps in is still adapted to the dark, which is a property no single-frame tone mapper has. (b) Learnable variant: expose 5-8 Retina scalars (sigma_adapt, dark_floor, gain, sigma_lamina, lamina_gain, lamina_floor) as parameters trained by the detection loss (RAOD/RAM style learnable nonlinearity), reporting their final values so that the branch cannot silently switch off (DRB lesson). (c) Per-candidate glare cue: on the per-track fovea (721 facets), the photoreceptor saturation fraction and L1/L2 contrast can serve as a flare-intensity covariate for confidence recalibration (Ma & Aelterman style LLR), but the project already showed that scene-state rescoring does not raise AUC (0.9025 -> 0.9005), so this is the weaker option. T4/T5 and the medulla are not needed for this item; what is used is only retina + lamina, i.e. the part of the connectome model that FlyVis itself lacks (FlyVis receives gray/255 without adaptation, retina.py docstring). Why meaningful: the fly photoreceptor compresses several log units of luminance and the LMCs re-centre contrast (Laughlin 1981) - the same job as HDR tone mapping, and the project already measured that this front-end repairs the fly's own night deficit (+0.009 [+0.005; +0.014] on dark videos, [flyvis_retina_stageB_v1↗](obsidian://open?vault=ObsidianFlyVis&file=01%20%D0%9C%D1%83%D1%85%D0%B0%2F%D0%AD%D0%BA%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D1%8B%2Fflyvis_retina_stageB_v1)) and that the gain is specific to the fly, not to DIS (dis_retina_control_v1), which is a warning that it may also not help YOLO.

## Какую потерю закрывает
Mainly THRESHOLD at night and on the overexposure subset: near headlights the object usually exists among candidates (blindness is 4.5% day and night alike), but bloom lowers local contrast and the score falls below threshold (21.0% night vs 15.5% day threshold losses, night score deficit +0.098). A local gain-control channel can restore contrast next to, but not inside, saturated regions. It does not address BLINDNESS caused by clipping: in 8-bit JPEG the saturated core carries no information, so RAW/HDR results do not transfer. Secondary: object birth for oncoming vehicles first visible as headlight pairs at distance (age-0 found 31% night vs 68% at age 10+). Small objects and occlusion are not targeted. Project priors are negative: the 'compressor' gate (dynamic range of layer-1 activations) showed no target (-0.0003 at a bar of 0.0115) and the quality map on the overexposed subset added +0.0003 at a bar of 0.0136; therefore the item must first prove that glare-adjacent GT is a large share of night threshold losses.

## Пересечение с чужими работами
Heavily occupied. Photoreceptor-inspired tone mapping: Reinhard & Devlin 2005. Insect photoreceptor + LMC front-end before motion detection: Brinkworth & O'Carroll 2009 (directly the model the project borrowed). Bio-inspired dark adaptation (pupil + photoreceptor simulation, gamma, fusion) for YOLO detection: Hung & Lin 2024. Photoreceptor layer inside CNNs: Idrees et al. 2024 (retina prediction, not detection). Learnable ISP for detection: RAW-Adapter, RAOD, RAM, GenISP, Dirty-Pixel. Illumination-invariant log/zero-sum input kernels that are essentially a lamina: YOLA 2024. Flare-aware confidence calibration on BDD100K: Ma & Aelterman 2024. No work was found that feeds a connectome-constrained (FlyVis) retina/lamina state or a temporally adapting photoreceptor stream into a modern detector [uncertain - absence of evidence is not a novelty argument]. cannot_claim: first bio-inspired photoreceptor adaptation for detection; first learnable tone mapping for detection; first illumination-invariant input module; first flare-aware detector; any HDR claim on BDD100K (it is 8-bit sRGB, no HDR content).

## Результаты у авторов
RAW-Adapter (RetinaNet; PASCALRAW, synthetic over-exposure and dark): ResNet-18 normal/over-exp/dark 88.7/88.7/82.5 mAP vs demosaicing 87.7/87.7/80.3 and Dirty-Pixel 88.6/88.0/80.8; ResNet-50 89.7/89.5/86.6 vs Dirty-Pixel 89.7/89.0/83.6 (default ISP normal 89.6). LOD (real low light, ResNet-50): RetinaNet 62.1 vs default ISP 58.4 (+3.7) and Dirty-Pixel 61.6; Sparse-RCNN 59.2 vs 53.9 (+5.3). Adapter 0.2-0.8M params vs Dirty-Pixel 4.28M. RAOD on ROD (24-bit Sony IMX490, day/night driving) as re-run by RAM (Faster R-CNN R18): ROD-Day mAP/mAP50 sRGB 24.5/40.1, RAOD 26.2/42.9, RAM 28.3/45.1; ROD-Night sRGB 38.9/62.8, RAOD 40.6/64.7, RAM 44.5/69.0 (+5.6 mAP over sRGB at night). Neural Exposure Fusion (CVPR 2024; Faster R-CNN with ResNet; automotive HDR Sony IMX490, 18 790 train, 1 996 test pairs; AP50): LDR learned AE (Onzon 2021) 41.1, single 20-bit Raw HDR 41.5, Deep HDR 41.9, PPNE 43.1, proposed local cross-attention fusion 44.2 (+2.7 over Raw HDR, +3.1 over learned LDR AE). Neural Auto-Exposure (CVPR 2021): +6.6 mAP over conventional auto-exposure in automotive HDR scenes. YOLA (NeurIPS 2024): ExDark mAP50 YOLOv3 71.0 -> 72.7, TOOD 72.5 -> 75.2; UG2+DarkFace YOLOv3 60.0 -> 61.5, TOOD 62.1 -> 67.4; COCO synthetic over-lit mAP 40.5 -> 41.2 (AP50 57.4 -> 58.3), well-lit 41.7 -> 42.3; learnable kernels beat fixed by 1.4 (ExDark) and 2.9 (DarkFace) mAP. Ma & Aelterman (BDD100K val with synthetic lens flare, YOLOv5m, post-hoc LLR recalibration by estimated flare intensity): about +0.1% AP over all objects, up to +1.5% AP for very severe flare on cars; all-class AP moderate flare 0.625 -> 0.634, severe 0.599 -> 0.614; traffic light severe 0.759 -> 0.784. 2PCNet (BDD100K day->night, Faster R-CNN R50, NightAug with glare insertion + two-phase teacher): night AP50 46.4 vs source-only 41.1 and night-supervised 46.2. AODRaw (Cascade R-CNN ConvNeXt-T): low-light AP 31.5 -> 32.1 with RAW pretraining. Hung & Lin 2024: +1.5 to 6% mAP from dark adaptation on a self-built simulated 0-lux-to-bright set (details [uncertain], full text not accessible). Frame rate and camera motion: ROD, LOD, PASCALRAW, ExDark are still images; Neural Exposure Fusion uses pairs of consecutive frames from a moving vehicle; none exploits a 30 Hz adaptation state.

## Проверка против сильной базы
Weak to moderate. Baselines are RetinaNet/Faster R-CNN/Sparse R-CNN with ResNet-18/50, YOLOv3, TOOD and YOLOv5m - none is YOLO11/YOLO26 class, and RAW/HDR gains come from extra bits that BDD100K does not have. Seeds and confidence intervals are mostly not reported (RAW-Adapter, YOLA, RAM give single numbers [uncertain for YOLA]). Same-information controls exist in some: RAW-Adapter vs default ISP and Dirty-Pixel; YOLA vs YOLA-Naive and enhancement-then-detect pipelines (KIND, Zero-DCE style) that often lose accuracy; Neural Exposure Fusion compares with Raw HDR given the same exposures. Independent reproduction: RAM (Sony) reproduces RAOD on ROD and confirms RAW > sRGB at night; nothing independent for YOLA or the flare-aware LLR. Over-exposure numbers are synthetic (PASCALRAW, COCO over-lit, synthetic flare on BDD). For this project the proper control is the same tensor shape without biology: Retina mode 'norm' (global normalisation) and CLAHE/log channels, same epochs and seeds; the project already showed with DIS that retina gains can be model-specific.

## Разрез по условиям
Over-exposure: PASCALRAW synthetic over-exp (RAW-Adapter), COCO synthetic over-lit (YOLA), synthetic lens-flare severity bins on BDD100K val (Ma & Aelterman). Night/low light: ROD-Night, LOD, ExDark, DarkFace, AODRaw low-light; BDD100K night only in 2PCNet (domain adaptation, AP per class). HDR scene categories (sunny, backlight, tunnel, dusk, night) in Neural Exposure Fusion but reported only as aggregate AP50. No breakdown found for real headlight glare on BDD100K, for small objects under glare, for occlusion, or for first appearance of an object.

## Дешёвая проверка
Gate 0 (CPU, 1-2 h, no training, decides whether the item lives): on runs/det_cache_ctl_960 (200 val videos, conf 0.01) plus runs/det_cache_over (30 overexposure videos) compute for every GT box: fraction of luminance >= 250 inside the box and in a ring of 1x box size, distance to the nearest saturated blob (>= 20 px), and local RMS contrast before vs after Retina 'lamina'. Rebuild the loss ladder (found / threshold-lost / blind) per glare bin, night vs day. Pre-registered rule: continue only if glare-adjacent GT (ring saturation >= 5% or blob within one box diagonal) accounts for >= 15% of night threshold losses AND its threshold-loss rate exceeds non-glare night GT by >= 5 pp; else close as 'no target' (consistent with the compressor gate). Gate 1 (GPU, ~1 h, diagnostic only): frozen best.pt at 960 on night val frames whose RGB is re-scaled by the Retina photoreceptor gain (out/in luminance ratio) vs Retina 'norm' vs CLAHE; record recall at the operating threshold on the glare bin; a frozen detector will suffer distribution shift, so a positive result (>= +2 pp on glare bin without >= 1 pp loss elsewhere) is encouraging, a negative one is not decisive. Gate 2 (GPU, ~2.5 h): fine-tune from weights/baseline_seed42/best.pt for 1 epoch at 640 (~21 min each): A = RGB, B = RGB + photoreceptor + lamina channels, C = RGB + two non-bio channels of identical shape (global norm + CLAHE luminance); 2 seeds each (6 runs). Pre-registered: B - C >= +0.006 night mAP50 (about 2x the control seed SD 0.0031) and B - A >= +0.006, glare-bin recall at threshold B - C >= +2 pp; plus a mechanical check that the new channel weights have non-zero gradient and non-negligible norm at the end (DRB lesson). Only if Gate 2 passes, run 12 epochs x 3 seeds.

## Стоимость у нас
Retina on full frames: ~4 ms/frame at 1280x720 with blur_downscale 4 (retina.py note), but the temporal adaptation needs the 30 Hz stream, so precompute by running each video sequentially: ~338k frames for 1400 train videos -> about 25-40 min CPU plus video decoding [uncertain]; store 2 extra uint8 channels for the ~56k labelled frames (a few GB). Ultralytics needs a custom 5-channel loader and must apply geometric augmentations identically to the extra channels and disable or replicate HSV augmentation (otherwise the bio channels contradict the RGB). Gate 2: ~2.5 h. Full test: 3 arms x 3 seeds x 12 epochs x ~4.6 h (slightly more input bandwidth) ~ 41 h; a 100-epoch confirmation of the winner vs control ~2 x 35 h. Fine-tune from best.pt is acceptable for gates, final claims need from-scratch or identical fine-tune schedules for both arms. Inference overhead: ~4 ms/frame CPU (or <1 ms on GPU) for the retina, negligible in the detector.

## Ожидаемый эффект
Most likely within noise. Night mAP50: 0 to +0.008 (best case about +2% of 0.3772), i.e. at best ~2.5 control SDs. Reasons: (1) RAW/HDR gains (+2.7 to +6.6 mAP) rely on unclipped bits that BDD100K JPEG lacks; (2) the closest sRGB analogue, YOLA, gives +0.7 AP on synthetic over-lit COCO and +1.7 to +2.7 mAP50 on ExDark against weak bases; (3) post-hoc flare-aware recalibration on BDD100K gives +0.1% AP overall; (4) project gates already found no dynamic-range target (-0.0003 at bar 0.0115) and no extra signal on the overexposed subset (+0.0003 at bar 0.0136). If Gate 0 shows that glare-adjacent GT is < 15% of night threshold losses, the ceiling is < 0.15 x 21% ~ 3 pp of night recall even with perfect repair, which would translate to DetA <= +0.3 and FN reduction <= ~2%. Expected DetA change: 0 to +0.2; FN: -0 to -1.5%.

## Риски
DRB lessons apply directly: seed noise 0.0144 mAP at 12 epochs is larger than the expected gain, so >= 3 seeds and bootstrap CIs are mandatory; sign flipped at 100 epochs before, so 12-epoch wins are not evidence for full schedules; a new branch may receive no gradient or be scaled to zero - log channel weight norms and, for the learnable variant, the Retina parameters; +17% time was a cost last time - here preprocessing is offline, but a 5-channel loader may slow data loading. Specific risks: augmentation inconsistency between RGB and precomputed bio channels; mosaic/mixup break the temporal adaptation state (adaptation must be computed on the original video, then cropped); distribution shift of pretrained first-layer weights; RAW-domain numbers are not comparable with sRGB BDD numbers and must not be cited as expected gains; synthetic over-exposure results may not reflect real headlight bloom.
