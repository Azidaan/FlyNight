# F19 Новая физическая информация: когда биоинспирация работает на уровне датчика (событийные камеры, HDR, RAW)

↑ [[07 Исследования/Фундаментальные проблемы/Фундаментальные проблемы|Фундаментальные проблемы]]

> Где биоинспирация реально выигрывает — на уровне датчика (события, HDR, RAW), а не постобработки RGB; чего нет в BDD100K; псевдособытия из кадров не несут новой информации; что из этого доступно нам.

## Механизм
Core idea: a deterministic re-processing of the same 8-bit frames cannot add information about the objects; it can only re-arrange it. Formally this is the data processing inequality (Cover and Thomas, Elements of Information Theory): for any function f, I(objects; f(frames)) <= I(objects; frames). A preprocessing stage (photoreceptor model, lamina, temporal averaging, pseudo-events, pseudo-RAW) can still help a model that is weak, small or mismatched to its input (inductive bias, easier optimisation, input brought into the training distribution), but it cannot help a strong learned detector that already sees the raw frames and has enough capacity, and it usually hurts a frozen detector because of input distribution shift. Bio-inspiration wins in the literature almost only where it changes what is physically measured, i.e. at the sensor: (1) Event cameras (silicon retinas, Lichtsteiner, Posch and Delbruck 2008; survey Gallego et al., TPAMI 2022) report per-pixel log-intensity changes asynchronously with microsecond resolution and >120 dB dynamic range versus ~60 dB for a standard frame camera; they measure light that a 30 Hz, 8-bit, auto-exposed frame never recorded (between-frame dynamics, contrast in saturated or very dark regions). (2) RAW sensor data keeps 12-24 bit linear photon counts before the ISP (demosaicing, denoising, tone mapping, gamma, 8-bit quantisation, video compression) throws information away; Xu et al. CVPR 2023 (ROD, 24-bit HDR RAW, day/night driving) and Li et al. CVPR 2025 (AODRaw) show that detection on RAW keeps the signal that sRGB clips. (3) HDR capture / exposure control (Onzon et al. CVPR 2021) chooses what light is recorded at all; a learned auto-exposure tuned for detection beats conventional auto-exposure by >6 mAP because it acts before saturation and quantisation. (4) Bio-inspired chips such as Tianmouc (Yang et al., Nature 2024; primate-like cognition + action pathways, 10,000 fps, 130 dB) gain because the pathway split is in silicon. The fly itself is a sensor-level system: R1-R6 photoreceptors adapt over many decades of light and use refractory sampling plus photomechanical microsaccades to gain hyperacuity (Juusola et al., eLife 2017); a model of that mechanism applied to an already tone-mapped, compressed 30 Hz video re-simulates the sensor on data whose information was already lost. Pseudo-events generated from frames (vid2e, Gehrig et al. CVPR 2020; v2e, Hu, Liu and Delbruck CVPRW 2021) are useful to pretrain networks that will later see REAL events, but they carry no information beyond the source frames: their timing is bounded by the frame rate (interpolation invents, it does not measure) and their contrast by the 8-bit tone-mapped pixel values. In v2e the reported night gain (+40% average accuracy for a car detector trained on v2e events versus YOLOv3 on intensity frames) comes at test time from a real DVS sensor, not from pseudo-events of the same frames [uncertain: exact test protocol]. Two more physical caveats: events also degrade in low light (DVS noise event rate and latency rise as photocurrent falls; Graca and Delbruck, IISW 2021), so the night advantage is real but not unlimited; and video compression (H.264 in BDD100K-like dashcam footage) temporally smooths and blocks dark regions, so even the 30 Hz temporal signal at night is partly codec output rather than photons.

## Где видно у нас
The project has run exactly the experiment this theory predicts. (1) Gate G3, card [fly_eye_frontend_v1↗](obsidian://open?vault=ObsidianFlyVis&file=01%20%D0%9C%D1%83%D1%85%D0%B0%2F%D0%AD%D0%BA%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D1%8B%2Ffly_eye_frontend_v1) (completed_negative, plan log 03.10.2026 15:18): frozen YOLO26 on frames after fly photoreceptor / temporal accumulation; recall at equal FP, variant minus raw, dev+test night (raw 0.7552): tavg3 -1.97 pp [-2.79; -1.22], tavg5 -5.69 pp [-7.06; -4.44], photoreceptor pr -9.45 pp [-11.54; -7.68], pr_int -15.13 pp [-17.20; -13.15]; day pr -2.88 pp; blindness at night grows raw 0.1047 -> pr 0.1585 -> pr_int 0.2167. Harm grows with the degree of change of the frame: this is distribution shift on top of zero new information. (2) The only place a bio front-end helped is where the consumer is weak/mismatched: retina-lamina before FlyVis removes the fly's night failure (+0.016 AP over geometry on dark videos, [flyvis_retina_stageB_v1↗](obsidian://open?vault=ObsidianFlyVis&file=01%20%D0%9C%D1%83%D1%85%D0%B0%2F%D0%AD%D0%BA%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D1%8B%2Fflyvis_retina_stageB_v1)) while the same retina before DIS gives +0.001 (dis_retina_control_v1). Ladder on dark videos: geometry+DIS 0.522, +fly-lamina 0.524, i.e. the gain is the fly being brought into its training distribution (FlyVis trained at brightness ~0.45 and contrast exp(N(0,0.2)); BDD night frames 13-30/255), not new information about objects. Before the retina the fly read compression block noise as motion (median |flow| on dark b1d22449 1.26 -> 0.36 px after the retina). (3) DRB in the YOLO neck: one branch weight = 0 in all 896 channels, frequency mask converged to neutral 4 of 4, seed spread 0.0144 mAP at 12 epochs, sign flipped at 100 epochs - consistent with a re-encoding of features the backbone already has. (4) Temporal information in 30 Hz BDD is real but small: tube - single +1.74% [+0.11; +3.18] of threshold-stage objects, motion relative to background adds +0.64% [-0.05; +1.38], net return of tube+motion -0.10% vs YOLO's own score (motion_rescore_30hz_v1). (5) The detector's night problem is not a pure 'no light' problem: blindness is 4.5% both night and day, threshold losses 21.0% night vs 15.5% day, and the car class has the same mAP night/day 0.817/0.816; 97% of the night mAP gap is rider/bus/motorcycle/bicycle. So the fraction of failures that can only be fixed by new photons (saturated headlights, black-crushed pedestrians) is bounded and not yet measured. (6) Final tracker: HOTA 58.47 -> 59.27 while DetA 58.21 -> 58.17 and FN 15,763 >> IDSw 2,221 - the bottleneck is detection, where sensor-level information would act, but BDD100K contains no RAW, no events, no exposure brackets: only 8-bit, tone-mapped, compressed 720p / 30 fps video recorded by phones [uncertain: per-video bitrate].

## Решения из литературы
What works in the literature is always 'new measurement + learned fusion', and the gains are largest exactly in the conditions the project targets. Events: RVT (CVPR 2023) reaches 47.2 mAP on Gen1 and 47.4 mAP on 1 Mpx with events only, <12 ms on a T4. DAGr (Nature 2024): a 20 Hz RGB CNN + asynchronous event GNN reaches the latency of a 5,000 Hz camera with the bandwidth of a 50 Hz camera (>100x bandwidth reduction) without losing accuracy; the gain is in the blind time between frames. PEOD (2025, 1280x720 pixel-aligned event-RGB, 340k boxes, 57% adverse): full test best RGB RT-DETR 21.8 mAP, best event SMamba 22.9, best fusion EOLO 26.1; on the illumination-challenge subset best RGB 13.2 mAP vs best event-only 23.2 mAP (+10.0 mAP, +76% relative) and current fusion models do not beat the event-only model there [uncertain: reported fusion number on that subset 11.5 mAP conflicts with the authors' statement that fusion beats RGB; check table]. PKU-DAVIS-SOD (346x260 DAVIS, normal / motion blur / low light): RGB+event fusion beats single modality, with the largest gains in low light and blur; SODFormer +7.1% mAP over NRE-Net [uncertain: which baseline modality]. Tracking: DSEC-MOT (SpikeMOT 2023) and FEMOT (2026): FEMOTR fusion HOTA 48.9 vs their RGB-only baseline 23.7 and event-only 17.7 on FEMOT; on DSEC-MOT the best RGB-event tracker Samba HOTA 55.4, FEMOTR 52.4 [uncertain: RGB-only baseline is the authors' own simplified variant, not a strong ByteTrack-class tracker; no night-only numbers given]. RAW: ROD (CVPR 2023, 24-bit HDR RAW, 4,053 day + 12,036 night images) shows dynamic-range adjustment of RAW is decisive (night AP 28.1 / AP50 47.0 when trained day+night). AODRaw (CVPR 2025, 7,785 RAW images, 9 conditions): sRGB 34.0 AP vs RAW pretraining 34.8 AP (+0.8 overall); per condition +0.3 normal, +0.9 low light, +4.8 rain, +2.2 fog - gains concentrate in adverse conditions but are only ~1 AP in low light against a strong ConvNeXt-T Cascade R-CNN. RAW-Adapter (ECCV 2024): learnable ISP adapters, relative gains 6.1% on LOD-synthetic, 3.1% on PASCALRAW+LOD, 9.8% on MultiRAW over strongest baselines. Ljungbergh et al. 2023: learnable non-linearity on RAW beats the RGB pipeline on PASCALRAW, qualitatively better in poor light. HDR / exposure: Onzon et al. 2021: detection-driven neural auto-exposure +6 mAP over conventional auto-exposure. Simulation route: vid2e / v2e convert video to pseudo-events; Stoffregen et al. 2020 show that matching contrast-threshold statistics improves sim-to-real by 20-40% for reconstruction and up to 15% for flow - but again only as training data for networks later fed real events. Unprocessing sRGB -> pseudo-RAW (Brooks et al. CVPR 2019; ReRAW 2025) likewise only creates training data for RAW sensors. Summary of strong-baseline numbers: new physical information buys roughly +1 AP (RAW, low light, strong detector) to +10 mAP (events, extreme illumination, mid-strength detectors); re-encoding the same frames buys ~0 against a strong detector, exactly as G3 measured (-2 to -15 pp for a frozen one).

## Что сделать с мухой
Separate two questions, because they need different data. A) Inside BDD100K (no new physical information available). Do not put the fly before the detector as a pixel front-end again: G3 already measured -9.45 pp at night for a frozen YOLO26, and theory says retraining can at best recover to raw (candidates K4/K5 in plan 7.6, literature 0...+1 pp). Keep the retina-lamina where it is useful: as the input adapter of FlyVis itself (it is the fly's own sensor stage and brings BDD night frames into the fly's training distribution, +0.016 AP measured). The only genuinely new signal in BDD relative to single-frame YOLO is 30 Hz time; let the fly consume time (its T4/T5 motion pathway) and be judged in the tracker/self-training loop (K1, K6), not as an image enhancer. Concretely: retina-lamina (Naka-Rushton, tau 0.3 s, sigma 24 px, gain 2, as in [flyvis_retina_stageB_v1↗](obsidian://open?vault=ObsidianFlyVis&file=01%20%D0%9C%D1%83%D1%85%D0%B0%2F%D0%AD%D0%BA%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D1%8B%2Fflyvis_retina_stageB_v1)) -> FlyVis with 734 free parameters trainable, topology/signs/synapse counts frozen (Lappalainen procedure) -> self-supervised next-frame / box-transfer objective on BDD train videos. B) Where bio-inspiration can actually win: give the fly a real event sensor. Event cameras are an electronic analogue of the fly's R1-R6 + lamina: log compression plus temporal contrast with ON/OFF polarity, which is what L1 (OFF-like) and L2 / L3 channels feed to T4 (ON) and T5 (OFF). Plan: (1) Data: DSEC (stereo 640x480 events + RGB, day and night, optical-flow ground truth, DSEC-Detection labels, DSEC-MOT tracks) - requires user permission for download. (2) Input mapping: bin events per hexagonal column (keep the ~13 px column scale fixed, as established) at the FlyVis integration step [uncertain: FlyVis dt, ~20 ms]; either (a) leaky integration of signed events per pixel = log-intensity estimate fed to R1-R6 (keeps FlyVis unchanged), or (b) ON/OFF counts injected directly as lamina drive (L1 <- OFF, L2/L3 <- ON-OFF), bypassing photoreceptors. Variant (a) is the minimal change and keeps a frozen-connectome comparison clean. (3) Training: freeze topology, train 734 free parameters + a <=1k-parameter input adapter on DSEC flow GT, with night sequences weighted; compare against E-RAFT-class event flow and against DIS on the paired RGB frames. (4) Control trio on the SAME scenes: fly on real events / fly on v2e pseudo-events made from DSEC RGB frames / fly on RGB frames through retina-lamina. Only the first minus the second is 'new physical information'; the second minus the third is just encoding. (5) If and only if the fly's event-driven motion beats DIS-on-RGB at night, plug it into the tracker on DSEC-MOT as the motion source / q_match signal (same XYWHByteTrack logic). For RAW/HDR there is no fly-specific advantage beyond photoreceptor adaptation, and no RAW video MOT dataset with tracks was found [uncertain]; RAW is best left as a literature argument. Note on framing: use DSEC only as a data source for the sensor-level question, without domain-specific claims.

## Тест до постройки
Three tests on data the project already has (val caches, det_cache night/day and det_cache_over for overexposure, 30 videos), none needs training. (1) Information-destruction audit (CPU, ~1-2 h): for every GT box in dev+test and the overexposure subset compute: fraction of luma pixels clipped (Y <= 2 or Y >= 253), number of distinct luma levels inside the box, local RMS contrast, and an 8x8 block-artifact energy (H.264 blocking). Split by detector outcome at equal FP: found / threshold loss / blind (not found even at conf 0.01). Fit a logistic model of 'lost' on these features with class x size x IoU controls exactly as in gate G0a (threshold_mechanism_iou_v1). Decision rule written in advance: if the night-minus-day gap in threshold+blind losses shrinks by >= 30% when conditioning on clipping/quantisation features, a sensor-level fix (HDR/RAW/events) has a real target in BDD-like data; if < 10%, the night failures are not caused by lost photons and no sensor-level module can help on BDD. (2) Inverse oracle on day frames (GPU ~1 h, frozen YOLO26): degrade day val frames only in dynamic range, matching night statistics (gain down to night mean 13-30/255, re-quantise to 8 bit, clip highlights at headlight levels, re-encode with H.264 at the same CRF), keep geometry and content identical. The recall drop at equal FP (day-degraded minus day) is the upper bound of what perfectly restored dynamic range could return at night; compare it with the measured night-day threshold-loss gap (21.0% vs 15.5%). (3) Temporal-information bound (already measured): tube - single +1.74% [+0.11; +3.18], tube+motion net -0.10%: 30 Hz BDD time adds little beyond YOLO's own score, so a fly-on-30Hz-video detector front-end has a ceiling near zero. Before an event study on DSEC: a 1-day probe - frozen FlyVis on DSEC night sequences with (i) real events via leaky integration, (ii) v2e pseudo-events from the paired frames, (iii) frames; linear readout of flow GT; decision: proceed only if (i) beats (ii) by more than the bootstrap CI over sequences.

## Потолок
Inside BDD100K the ceiling of any sensor-level bio-inspiration is zero new information by construction: BDD stores only post-ISP, 8-bit, compressed 30 Hz video; pseudo-events, pseudo-RAW or a photoreceptor model computed from it are functions of the same frames (data processing inequality). The best a learned re-encoding can do is match raw input; measured frozen-detector effect is -2 to -15 pp. The upper bound for what real new photons could have given at night is bounded by the night-specific loss: blindness is equal night and day (4.5%), so the night-specific part is the threshold-loss excess 21.0 - 15.5 = 5.5 pp of night GT objects, of which ~40% [33; 51] is already explained by box inaccuracy (G0a); the remaining ~3.3 pp of night recall is the maximum target, and only the clipped / quantised share of it (to be measured by diagnostic 1) is reachable by HDR/RAW/events [uncertain: share]. In DetA terms this is likely <= +1 DetA overall because night boxes are a minority and car class is already equal day/night [uncertain]. Off BDD, literature ceilings: events +10 mAP vs best RGB on extreme-illumination subset (PEOD, 13.2 -> 23.2), RAW +0.9 AP low light / +0.8 AP overall against a strong sRGB detector (AODRaw), +6 mAP for detection-aware exposure control (Onzon 2021).

## Где обязано помогать
Must help: night (BDD timeofday=night; brightness < 60/255 almost coincides with 27 of 29 night videos), overexposure subset (30 videos, det_cache_over: headlights, exits from shade), and objects with clipped or crushed pixels inside the box (from diagnostic 1); in DSEC: night sequences and fast-motion/blur segments. Must not harm: day well-exposed scenes (recall at equal FP within CI of zero; G3 showed day harm -2.06 to -2.88 pp for fixed front-ends), car class (already 0.817/0.816 night/day), and tracker identity metrics (IDSw, AssA). Always report night, day, overexposure, age-0 (first appearance) separately, with bootstrap over videos/sequences, and use only unseen videos (plan rule 6.13).

## Отрицательный контроль
1) Same-information control: pseudo-events (v2e / vid2e) generated from the very frames the RGB branch sees, run through the identical fly pipeline; any gain that survives this control is encoding, not new physical information, and cannot be credited to the sensor. 2) Non-biological front-end with the same information: log transform + temporal difference + learned 1x1 conv with equal parameter count (the analogue of tavg3 / homography average in G3); credits the fly only if fly > this. 3) Shuffled connectome (as in [fly_connectome_null_v1↗](obsidian://open?vault=ObsidianFlyVis&file=01%20%D0%9C%D1%83%D1%85%D0%B0%2F%D0%AD%D0%BA%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D1%8B%2Ffly_connectome_null_v1): shuffled weights keeping counts) on the same events. 4) Matched consumer control: the same retina/event input given to DIS or E-RAFT (as [dis_retina_control_v1↗](obsidian://open?vault=ObsidianFlyVis&file=01%20%D0%9C%D1%83%D1%85%D0%B0%2F%D0%AD%D0%BA%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D1%8B%2Fdis_retina_control_v1) did) - if DIS gains equally, the gain is the input, not the fly. 5) Modality ablation at test time (events zeroed, RGB zeroed). 6) For RAW: unprocessed pseudo-RAW from sRGB (Brooks 2019) versus real RAW on the same scenes. 7) Distribution-shift control for any frozen consumer: fine-tune the consumer on the processed input for the same number of steps as the treatment (K5) so that 'harm' from shift is separated from 'no information'.

## Вклад ветви
Event/sensor branch fused through a zero-initialised scalar or per-channel gate; log per epoch: gate norm |alpha| (DRB failure mode: weight = 0 in all 896 channels), ratio of gradient norms event-branch / RGB-branch at the fusion point, test-time modality dropout (metric with events zeroed and with RGB zeroed) every 2 epochs, and linear CKA between fused features and RGB-only features (CKA -> 1 means the branch is ignored). For the fly: norm of change of the 734 free parameters from initialisation and the fraction of T4/T5 activity variance explained by event input versus frame input.

## Минимальный эффект и стоимость
BDD diagnostics: audit 1-2 h CPU at Idle priority; inverse oracle ~1 h GPU (frozen YOLO26, cached val). MDE from project numbers: seed spread 0.0144 mAP at 12 epochs; two-arm comparison, alpha 0.05, power 0.8: MDE ~ 2.8 x 0.0144 x sqrt(2/n) = 0.033 mAP at n = 3 seeds per arm, 0.025 at n = 5 (remember the Ultralytics seed must be wired into the dataloader manually, rule 6.12). At 4.3 h per 12-epoch run, 3+3 runs ~ 26 h, 5+5 ~ 43 h of RTX 5070 time. Bootstrap MDE over videos for recall at equal FP is ~1 pp at night on dev+test (G3 CIs were +-0.8 to +-2 pp). DSEC branch: download ~ hundreds of GB for events + frames [uncertain: exact size; ask user before downloading]; FlyVis on 640x480 events at 1/16 density ~1.2 ms per step on RTX 5070 (measured project figure for frames), a 1-day frozen probe and a 1-2 day 734-parameter fine-tune on flow GT fit in 12 GB; the number of DSEC night test sequences is small, so MDE over sequences will be large (several mAP or ~0.5 px EPE) [uncertain]; training an RVT-class event detector from scratch is multi-day and out of budget for a master's thesis - use public checkpoints.

## Вердикт для проекта
Important structural explanation, secondary as a cause of the main failures. It is the main explanation of G3 (and of why the retina helps only the fly, +0.016, and not DIS, +0.001): on BDD100K any bio-inspired pixel/temporal front-end re-encodes information YOLO26 already has, so it can only lose (shift) or tie. It is not the main reason DetA does not move: the dominant losses (threshold losses, rare classes rider/bus/motorcycle/bicycle, FN 15,763 vs IDSw 2,221) are learning/ambiguity problems in information that is present, and BDD contains no RAW, events or HDR to change that. Priority: (1) high and cheap - run the information-destruction audit and the inverse oracle (1 day) to put a number on the 'lost photons' share; this closes the sensor-level question for BDD with evidence rather than argument; (2) medium - keep the fly's own sensor stage (retina-lamina) and move the fly to where BDD has new signal (time) via K1; (3) optional chapter - the DSEC real-events vs pseudo-events vs frames comparison for FlyVis, which is the one setting where the fly's biology (log-temporal-contrast input) matches a real sensor and literature shows +10 mAP-scale gains in extreme light; needs download permission. Cannot claim: that pseudo-events or photoreceptor models add information on BDD; that events help tracking at night on BDD; any novelty from the negative G3 result.
