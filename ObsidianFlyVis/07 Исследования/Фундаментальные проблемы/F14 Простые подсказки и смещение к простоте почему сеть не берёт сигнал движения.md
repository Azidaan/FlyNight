# F14 Простые подсказки и смещение к простоте: почему сеть не берёт сигнал движения

↑ [[07 Исследования/Фундаментальные проблемы/Фундаментальные проблемы|Фундаментальные проблемы]]

> Shortcut learning (Geirhos 2020, Puli NeurIPS 2023): ERM предпочитает простую предсказательную подсказку (текстура) стабильной (движение); управление отступом, обучение на подмножестве, где текстура не работает.

## Механизм
Shortcut learning (Geirhos et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2020): networks trained by empirical risk minimisation (ERM) adopt whichever decision rule is easiest to reach and predictive on the training distribution, not the one intended by the designer. Several distinct mechanisms produce this. (1) Simplicity bias (Shah et al., NeurIPS 2020): SGD-trained nets rely exclusively on the simplest predictive feature and can stay invariant to more complex, equally predictive features. (2) Availability vs predictivity (Hermann et al., ICLR 2024): which feature is used depends not only on how predictive it is but on how 'available' (easy to extract, large amplitude) it is; a less available but more predictive feature can be ignored. (3) Margin/gradient mechanism (Puli et al., NeurIPS 2023): even when a stable feature determines the label perfectly and the shortcut adds no information, gradient descent on cross-entropy drifts towards the max-margin solution, which loads on the shortcut; uniform-margin losses (MARG-CTRL) remove this. (4) Gradient starvation (Pezeshki et al., NeurIPS 2021): once one feature fits the training data, the loss gradient for the others vanishes, so they are never learned. (5) Modality imbalance in multi-branch networks (Wang, Tran & Feiszli, CVPR 2020; Peng et al., CVPR 2022): in joint training of two streams, the dominant stream fits faster, suppresses the gradient into the weaker stream, and the joint model can even be worse than the best single stream; the uni-modal encoders inside a jointly trained model are under-optimised relative to their separately trained counterparts. (6) In video specifically, spatiotemporal networks are biased toward static appearance: Kowal et al. (CVPR 2022) quantified that most action-recognition models encode mostly static information and that some datasets assumed to need dynamics are actually solvable statically. For the project, 'texture/appearance from one frame' is the high-availability cue (YOLO backbone already extracts it, its features are large and pretrained), while 'motion' (from the fly, DIS or frame stacks) is a low-availability cue whose information is largely redundant with appearance on the in-distribution training set (cars by day). The combination of redundancy + lower availability + a pretrained dominant branch is exactly the regime where (3)–(5) predict that an added motion branch gets near-zero weight, and that its contribution appears, if at all, only on the subset where appearance fails (night, glare, occlusion) — which is a minority of training frames.

## Где видно у нас
Consistent with the mechanism: (1) DRB in the YOLO neck: one branch weight w_lqa = exactly 0 in all 896 channels and the frequency mask converged to neutral in 4 of 4 runs; frequency-branch weight moved by only 0.002 in 12 epochs (plan [[05 План/2. Хронология|§2]]). Caveat: the zero weight was partly a wiring defect (the gated branch received no gradient, plan 25.08), so it is not clean evidence of simplicity bias; the neutral frequency mask (4/4) is the cleaner sign of gradient starvation. (2) Fly as identity signature: +0.14 AP to geometry alone, but on top of the eye input (721 facets) only +0.006 [+0.001; +0.012]; the fly alone is worse than its own input (−0.052) — the head takes the more available raw input and the fly adds almost nothing ([[fly_matched_controls_v1]]). (3) Appearance and fly on top of geometry stitching: +0.08 and ~0 HOTA, CI through zero, while geometry alone +0.79 HOTA ([[stitch_appearance_v1]]) — the simplest cue (box geometry) absorbs the available gain. (4) Motion rescoring of weak candidates at 5 Hz and 30 Hz, tubelet + motion relative to background: ≈0 at equal FP (−0.10% [−3.80; +3.01] dev+test; motion over tubelet +0.64% [−0.05; +1.38]) — YOLO's own one-frame score already contains what box-level motion provides (plan rule 6.14). (5) Retraining the confidence target without IoU: 0 (−0.15 pp night) — the network keeps its own score logic. (6) Fusion head with fly-lamina in the tracker: ΔHOTA −0.014 [−0.037; +0.016] ([[e2e_motion_source_v1]]). Against the mechanism as the main cause: (a) the fly motion signal itself is poor (52.77 px error vs 4.87 px for DIS), so there is no strong stable motion feature being ignored — the network is not 'refusing' a good cue; (b) DIS motion, which is accurate, also gave ΔHOTA ≈ 0 in the tracker beyond the IoU buffer; (c) the main loss is detection (DetA 58.21 → 58.17 unchanged, FN 15 763 ≫ IDSw 2 221), where no motion branch has been trained into the detector yet. (d) Night is the natural 'minority group': threshold losses 21.0% night vs 15.5% day; blindness 4.5% both — the night-specific part is the score, which is where a cue the network ignores on day frames could help.

## Решения из литературы
(1) Margin control (Puli 2023): replacing the cross-entropy objective by uniform-margin losses raises test worst-group accuracy over default ERM (two seeds): Waterbirds 70.8 → 75.9–80.5 (SD 80.5 ± 1.4), CelebA 72.8 → 79.4–82.8 (marg-log 82.8 ± 1.1), CivilComments 60.1 → 67.8–69.9; at the same training time as ERM, while JTT takes ~6/80/120 and CNC ~8/180/360 min per epoch on the three datasets. (2) Re-training only the last layer on a group-balanced or shift-specific subset (DFR, Kirichenko 2023): ERM features already contain the core feature; re-weighting them gives worst-group 92.0 ± 0.9 on Waterbirds and 88.0 ± 1.6 on CelebA (released checkpoints), versus ~70–73 for ERM (Puli Table 1). (3) Upweighting the examples the first model gets wrong (JTT, Liu 2021): in Puli's reproduction worst-group 71.7 ± 4.0 on Waterbirds and 75.2 ± 4.6 on CelebA, i.e. weaker than margin control. (4) Modality balancing: OGM-GE on CREMA-D (audio-visual) accuracy 51.7 (concatenation) → 61.9, Kinetics-Sounds 59.8 → 62.3; Gradient-Blending 56.8 on CREMA-D; modality dropout 53.3–54.4. Mechanism: monitor each branch's contribution and scale down the gradient of the dominant branch. (5) Train the weak stream alone first (uni-modal pre-training / separate losses per stream), then fuse — the under-optimisation shown by OGM-GE disappears if each branch has its own loss. (6) Deliberately build the training subset where the simple cue fails (cue-conflict / minority group) and evaluate there; Kowal 2022 show two-stream architectures with cross-connections balance static and dynamic information better than single-stream 3D CNNs. Caveat: all numbers are on classification benchmarks with a known spurious attribute; transfer to detection/MOT is not demonstrated in these papers [uncertain].

## Что сделать с мухой
1) Give the fly its own objective before fusion, and fuse late. Train the fly readout (and, if allowed, the 734 parameters + retina-lamina) on a task only motion can solve: e.g. predict the YOLO box displacement between frames (K1 in plan [[05 План/7.6. Отбор следующих экспериментов по схеме RPM, 03.10|§7.6]]), or same/different object on pairs that have SIMILAR appearance (hard negatives where the eye-input head fails). Only after that, freeze the fly and add it to the YOLO/tracker head. 2) Target the residual, not the label: train the fly head to predict the errors of the existing system (e.g. which below-threshold candidates are true at night; which stitching links of the geometry stitcher are wrong). A residual target removes the redundant part that the dominant cue already explains, so gradient starvation cannot occur. 3) If the fly is inserted as a branch into YOLO (candidates K4/K9), use OGM-GE-style gradient modulation or a per-branch auxiliary detection loss, and zero-initialised residual fusion with a scalar gate whose value is logged (and ablated α = 0 at inference). Freeze YOLO for the first epochs so the fly branch is not starved, then unfreeze. 4) DFR-style last-layer retraining on the night/glare/occluded subset: keep YOLO and fly features frozen, retrain only the classification/score head on a group-balanced mix (night-dominated) — cheap and directly tests whether the fly features carry information that ERM did not weight. 5) Use a margin-control loss (σ-damp or spectral decoupling, SD) for the fly-fused score head; both are a few lines of change to the loss.

## Тест до постройки
All on existing caches, no detector training. (1) Residual probe: fit a logistic/linear probe on fly features to predict the residual of the current best head (e.g. errors of geometry + eye-input head on pair AP, or FN-at-threshold of YOLO at night); compare with the same probe on matched controls (eye input, shuffled connectome). If the fly predicts the residual significantly better than the controls (video-bootstrap CI > 0), there is a stable cue being starved; if not, the problem is absence of signal, not shortcut learning. (2) Cue-conflict subset: from the identity pairs, select pairs where appearance similarity is high for different objects or low for the same object (appearance fails); measure fly-alone AP vs input-alone AP there. The fly should be better than the input on this subset; currently fly alone is −0.052 vs input on all pairs. (3) Availability check (Hermann 2024): train the same small head on each cue alone and record epochs-to-reach-90%-of-final; a cue that is predictive but slow to learn is a starvation candidate. (4) Branch-alone vs joint: train the fusion head with the fly branch alone, input alone, and joint; if joint ≤ best single (the OGM-GE symptom), modality imbalance is present.

## Потолок
For association, the measured accessible ceiling is small: geometry stitching +0.79 HOTA (+1.4% of 58.47), and everything appearance-like beyond it ≤ +0.08 (n.s.); the oracle association headroom (+45% IDF1) is mostly not reachable by any cue. A perfectly balanced motion branch in the tracker is therefore bounded at about +0.1–0.3 HOTA [uncertain]. For detection, the target is the night threshold loss: 21.0% vs 15.5% day, i.e. ~5.5 pp of night objects lost at the threshold that are not lost by day; if a fly cue recovered all of this excess at equal FP, night recall would rise by ≈5.5 pp × (share of night objects at threshold) — the upper bound on the gain attributable to 'a cue that works when texture fails' [uncertain]. Box-level motion already measured ≈0 (Г0б-30), so the remaining ceiling must come from pixel-level, pre-detector motion, which is unmeasured.

## Где обязано помогать
Where texture fails: night videos (threshold losses 21.0%), glare, occluded objects (71.1% of object-frames have occlusion attributes), age-0 objects (first appearance; recall at night age 0 is 0.389 vs 0.654 for all night objects in [[conf_target_probe_v1]]), and appearance-ambiguous identity pairs. No-harm check: day videos and car class (car mAP night/day 0.817/0.816) must stay within the CI of the baseline; DetA overall must not fall.

## Отрицательный контроль
(1) The same branch fed with the fly's own input (721-facet eye image) without the network — a gain must exceed this (matched-input control, as in [[fly_matched_controls_v1]]). (2) Shuffled connectome with preserved degrees and signs. (3) The same balancing/margin trick applied to a non-fly second stream (e.g. DIS flow or a frame-difference stream) — if the trick lifts DIS equally, the gain belongs to the trick, not to the fly. (4) The balancing trick applied to the baseline without any second branch (e.g. margin-control loss on YOLO score head alone) — controls for the loss change itself. (5) Temporal shuffle of the fly input frames (destroys motion while keeping appearance statistics) — proves the gain comes from motion.

## Вклад ветви
Log per epoch: the scalar gate/residual α of the fly branch; L2 norm of fly-branch weights vs main-branch weights; ratio of gradient norms ||∇fly|| / ||∇main|| (the OGM-GE discrepancy ratio of per-branch softmax scores); validation metric with the fly branch zeroed at inference (ablation per epoch). A starved branch shows α → 0 and gradient ratio → 0 early (the DRB pattern: weight shift 0.002 in 12 epochs).

## Минимальный эффект и стоимость
Diagnostics (residual probe, cue-conflict AP, availability) are CPU-minutes on existing caches. Head-only retraining (DFR-style) on frozen YOLO + fly caches: minutes–1 h. A balanced fly branch inside YOLO: 12 epochs ≈ 4.3 h per run on the RTX 5070; with the measured short-run seed spread 0.0144 mAP, ≥3 seeds per arm (≈26 h for 2 arms) still leave an MDE ≈ 0.03 mAP (see F15), which is larger than any expected effect — so in-detector branch experiments should be preceded by the head-only tests. MDE for head-level tests: recall at equal FP has paired CI half-width ≈0.17–0.2 pp ([[conf_target_probe_v1]] night), MDE ≈ 0.25 pp; pair AP on top of input CI half-width ≈0.005, MDE ≈ 0.007.

## Вердикт для проекта
Secondary. Simplicity bias / modality imbalance explains part of the pattern (DRB neutral frequency mask 4/4, fly adding only +0.006 over its input, appearance over geometry n.s.), and it is a real risk for any future fly branch inside YOLO. But the dominant reason the network does not use the fly's motion is that the fly's motion signal is weak (52.77 px vs 4.87 px for DIS) and that even accurate motion (DIS, box-level motion) adds ≈0 on top of YOLO's own score — i.e. absence of complementary information more than starvation of present information. Priority: medium. Do the residual probe and cue-conflict subset first (hours, CPU); if they show a starved fly signal, apply separate fly loss + late fusion + gradient modulation; if not, put effort into improving the fly signal itself (K1 fine-tuning on BDD video).
