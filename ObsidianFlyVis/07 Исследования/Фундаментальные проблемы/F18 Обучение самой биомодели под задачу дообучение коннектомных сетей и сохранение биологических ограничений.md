# F18 Обучение самой биомодели под задачу: дообучение коннектомных сетей и сохранение биологических ограничений

↑ [[07 Исследования/Фундаментальные проблемы/Фундаментальные проблемы|Фундаментальные проблемы]]

> Как дообучать FlyVis (734 параметра, сетчатку) на видео вождения или задаче опознания; самообучение (предсказание кадра, фотометрическая ошибка); что остаётся «мушиным»; работы по дообучению коннектомных/биоограниченных сетей 2023–2026.

## Механизм
1) What is actually trainable in FlyVis. In the released recipe (tools/flyvis-runtime/flyvis/config) the connectome fixes the graph, the synapse counts (SynapseCount, requires_grad: false) and the synapse signs; the 734 free parameters are 65 resting potentials (one per cell type), 65 membrane time constants (one per type, initial 0.05 s) and 604 non-negative synapse-strength scale factors grouped by (source type, target type) (SynapseCountScaling, clamp non_negative). Training is Adam, lr 5e-5 decayed stepwise to 5e-6, batch 4, 250 000 iterations, 19-frame Sintel clips at dt = 0.02 s, an L2 optic-flow loss through a small convolutional decoder (DecoderGAVP, dropout 0.5) plus an activity penalty (baseline 5.0, applied until iteration 150 000). Augmentation is mild: contrast SD 0.2, brightness SD 0.1, Gaussian noise 0.08, no gamma. Lappalainen et al. (Nature 2024; preprint bioRxiv 2023.03.11.532232) report that without connectome constraints the same architecture would need >2 billion parameters, that models with lower task error had more realistic tuning (DSI/FRI prediction, p = 5.6e-6), and that the best model predicted ON/OFF polarity correctly for 29 of 31 characterised types. 2) Why the frozen Sintel model fails on BDD100K night video. (a) Input-distribution gap: night frames are dark, low-SNR, JPEG-blocky and contain point light sources - far outside the augmentation range; the project measured that the fly took compression block noise for motion on the dark video (median |flow| 1.26 px -> 0.36 px after the retina-lamina front-end). (b) Task/scale gap: the decoder was trained to output Sintel flow on a 721-column hexagonal lattice whose column spacing corresponds to ~13 px in the project's sampling; box motion of road scenes at 30 Hz and the ego-motion field are not what the decoder learned, so even a perfect internal representation can be decoded badly. (c) Only 734 shared parameters: the network has per-type, not per-cell, freedom, so fine-tuning can change gains and time constants of whole types (e.g. longer integration in photoreceptors/lamina at night, which is exactly what real flies do) but cannot learn new spatial features. 3) What fine-tuning can and cannot preserve. Because the graph, counts and signs are fixed, any fine-tuned model stays 'connectome-constrained' by construction; what can be lost is the functional fly-likeness (T4/T5 direction selectivity, ON/OFF segregation, flash response polarity). Karaneen, Schomburg and Chklovskii (bioRxiv 2026.04.18.717873) retrained two new 50-model ensembles with the unchanged public recipe and found that task error does not reliably select the biologically plausible solution: validation errors of clusters with and without realistic T4c tuning overlap, and lowest-error clusters did not consistently match experimental tuning. So 'better task loss' and 'more fly-like' are only loosely coupled, and a task-driven fine-tune can drift to a non-fly solution with similar or better loss. Self-supervised, activity-based objectives are a better match to biology: Qin et al. (arXiv 2512.23146, 2025) showed that a local self-supervised predictive (past-future CCA) rule on translating natural scenes produces L1/L2/L3-like temporal filters with SNR adaptation and T4-like direction selectivity whose weights approximate connectome-derived patterns. 4) Fine-tuning literature in deep learning: penalising the distance to the pretrained parameters (L2-SP, Li, Grandvalet, Davoine ICML 2018) or weighting it by Fisher information (EWC, Kirkpatrick et al. PNAS 2017) is the standard way to adapt while keeping the source solution; with only 734 parameters, Fisher matrices are cheap and exact.

## Где видно у нас
(1) Motion: frozen FlyVis decoded motion error 52.77 px versus 4.87 px for DIS (plan section 7.6, K1), FlyVis tracker HOTA 28.12-29.11 versus ByteTrack 52.44 ([[flyvis_stress]]), motion_confidence anti-informative in 13 of 14 video x mode combinations ([[motion_confidence_audit]]), tracker delta HOTA ~0 when the fly is used as motion source. (2) The representation is not the bottleneck in the way one might hope: internal T4/T5 readout is worse than the decoded flow (AP lower by 0.0045, CI fully negative, [[flyvis_internal_readout_v1]]) and indistinguishable from shuffled readout; the nonlinear (HGB) readout finds nothing either ([[flyvis_readout_nonlinear_diag]]). (3) Input adaptation alone helps the fly specifically: Naka-Rushton photoreceptor + lamina contrast normalisation turned the fly from harmful to useful in the reliability head (geometry + fly 0.393 -> 0.414 versus geometry 0.397; dark video -0.027 -> +0.011; fly decoder calibration coefficient g 0.398 -> 0.243), and ended the night dip (+0.016) while the same front-end does nothing for DIS (+0.0012) - evidence that the fly's failure at night is partly an input-statistics problem that a trainable front-end and trainable time constants could address. (4) Re-identification: the fly as a signature is worse than its own 721-facet input (-0.0521 [-0.0765; -0.0295] test) and adds only +0.0060 on top - a symptom of a model that was never trained to preserve object identity. (5) Rewired null keeps trained parameters (tracking/connectome_null.py), so the current +0.044 AP 'topology' effect cannot tell whether a fine-tuned fly would keep any topology advantage. (6) Constraints of the plan: FlyVis is the permanent subject; plan 7.6 candidate K1 (fine-tune the 734 parameters + retina on BDD100K by next-frame / box-transfer self-supervision) is estimated at 3-5 GPU-hours plus 1-2 days of code; K2 (train the fly for same/different object) at 2-4 GPU-hours.

## Решения из литературы
A. Self-supervised flow on unlabelled video (closest to K1). Photometric-loss methods train flow without labels: SMURF (CVPR 2021) reaches Sintel test EPE 3.15 clean / 4.18 final and KITTI-2015 Fl-all 6.83 % without ground truth, i.e. at the level of supervised RAFT trained on synthetic data. At night the brightness-constancy assumption is broken; UnFlow (AAAI 2018) uses a census-transform loss that is robust to illumination changes, and Zheng et al. (CVPR 2020) show that flow networks trained on synthetic low-light noise degrade much less in the dark than standard ones [uncertain: exact EPE numbers not re-verified]. For FlyVis, the target is not to beat DIS (4.87 px) but to make the fly's motion output reliable where DIS is not; supervision can come for free from DIS/RAFT computed on the daytime-like or retina-normalised frame (distillation), from photometric warping of the next frame, or from tracker boxes (box-transfer: the decoded flow inside a box must move the box to its next GT/tracked position). B. Activity-based / predictive self-supervision that is biologically plausible: Qin et al. 2025 (past-future CCA, local rule) recover L1/L2/L3 temporal filters with SNR adaptation and T4-like DS tuning with connectome-like weights; Karaneen et al. 2026 and their citation of Duan et al. 2025 argue that activity-based objectives stabilise biologically plausible solutions better than task error. For FlyVis this means a next-frame-prediction head on photoreceptor/lamina/T4-T5 activity, rather than an object-level loss pushed through all 734 parameters. C. Regularised fine-tuning that keeps the pretrained solution: L2-SP (ICML 2018) consistently beat plain fine-tuning in transfer (e.g. several points of accuracy on small target datasets) [uncertain: numbers not re-verified for this item]; EWC protects parameters with high Fisher information. With 734 parameters one can compute the exact Fisher of the Sintel loss and of the fly-likeness metrics, and penalise movement along those directions. D. Model ensembles and selection by biology, not only by task: Lappalainen trained 50 models and used the top 20 % by validation error; Karaneen et al. show that this selection is unstable, so the fine-tuned model must be selected with explicit biological criteria (DSI and FRI correlation with experiments, ON/OFF polarity of 31 characterised types) in addition to task loss. E. Training with behavioural / downstream objectives while keeping unit identity: Cowley et al. 2024 (knockout training) trained a model of 23 LC types from courtship behaviour of 459 fly pairs and still mapped units to real neurons (model-neuron correlation ~35 % reported in press material [uncertain]); this is the precedent for training a fly-structured model with a task loss without losing the unit-to-cell-type mapping. F. Rate-model analysis of what connectome constraints can predict: Beiran and Litwin-Kumar (Nat Neurosci 2025) show that connectivity alone underdetermines single-neuron parameters and that prediction requires recordings from a subset of neurons - so a task-only fine-tune can move parameters substantially while the graph stays fixed [uncertain: summary from abstract].

## Что сделать с мухой
Staged plan that keeps the fly a fly. Stage 0 (no fly training): train only the input front-end (Naka-Rushton photoreceptor adaptation tau, sigma, gain; lamina normalisation) and the decoder on BDD100K 30 Hz clips with a distillation target (DIS or RAFT flow resampled to the hex lattice, on frames where those are reliable) and with a photometric census loss on the next frame; fly parameters frozen. This separates 'input statistics' from 'fly dynamics'. Stage 1 (fly time constants and resting potentials only, 130 parameters): unfreeze the 65 tau and 65 V_rest, keep the 604 synapse scales frozen; this is the biologically plausible adaptation (real photoreceptor/lamina integration lengthens in dim light). Add L2-SP to the Sintel solution and keep a 20-30 % Sintel batch mix so Sintel EPE must stay within the 50-model ensemble range. Stage 2 (all 734): only if stage 1 gains and still passes fly-likeness. Losses: (i) flow distillation + census photometric loss through the existing decoder; (ii) optional box-transfer loss for motion inside detector boxes; (iii) activity penalty as in the recipe; (iv) for re-identification (K2) do not push a contrastive loss through the 734 parameters first - train a readout on the frozen fine-tuned fly, since the measured reid ceiling is small. Fly-likeness gate after every checkpoint (all available locally in flyvis.analysis): dsi_correlation_to_known and fri_correlation_to_known on moving edges and flashes, ON/OFF polarity agreement over the 31 characterised types, T4/T5 preferred-direction angular distance to known; reject checkpoints that drop below the 10th percentile of the original 50-model ensemble. Controls (F17): the same fine-tune on a degree-and-sign-preserving rewired graph from the same initialisation (C8), a parameter-matched non-biological recurrent network with the same input lattice and ~734 + decoder parameters, and the frozen fly with only the front-end trained (stage 0). Freeze the detector (YOLO26) throughout; the fly is evaluated where it has measured headroom: motion reliability head on dark videos and q_match after loss.

## Тест до постройки
Before writing training code: (1) Decoder-only oracle on existing caches: train only a new linear/conv decoder from cached FlyVis activity (all 65 types, existing runs/flyvis_activity) to DIS flow on BDD clips; if the decoded error stays far above DIS (e.g. > 2x DIS 4.87 px) even with a decoder trained on BDD, the representation (not the Sintel decoder) is the limit and fly fine-tuning is needed; if it approaches DIS, stage 0 suffices and the 52.77 px is a decoder/scale artefact. (2) Linear probe of activity for DIS flow residual on dark versus bright videos (same split as [[flyvis_retina_stageB_v1]]) - tells whether night failure is in the photoreceptor/lamina stage (probe on early types) or later (T4/T5). (3) Gradient sanity: one forward/backward pass on 19-frame BDD clips at dt 0.02 s, record gradient norm per parameter group (tau, V_rest, synapse scales) and activity saturation per type; if gradients concentrate on a few types, restrict training to them. (4) Fisher/sensitivity: exact diagonal Fisher of Sintel loss and of DSI correlation over the 734 parameters (cheap) to see whether the directions the BDD loss wants to move are the ones that carry fly-likeness. (5) CKA between fly activity and the 721-facet input on BDD crops: if CKA is ~1 for the readout used in reid, the fly is acting as an input copy and a task fine-tune is the only way to add information. Costs: (1)-(2) CPU/GPU minutes on caches, (3)-(4) < 1 GPU-hour.

## Потолок
Motion: if the fine-tuned fly matched DIS (4.87 px), the measured tracker-level value is still bounded by what DIS already gives - DIS adds nothing to q_match ([[event_heads_v1]]) and the tracker DetA is unchanged by association changes (58.21 -> 58.17); geometry-only stitching gives +0.79 HOTA and other cues add non-significant amounts. So the realistic ceiling of a better fly motion source is the share of HOTA tied to motion reliability at night and occlusion re-acquisition, roughly 0-0.5 HOTA [uncertain: extrapolated from the stitching ceiling]. In the motion-reliability head the gap from geometry+DIS (0.5215 dark) to a perfect reliability oracle is large, but the fly over DIS currently adds +0.0032 [-0.0013; +0.0078] dark with retina-DIS; a well-trained fly could plausibly reach the size of DIS's own gain over geometry (+0.07 AP) only if it carries information DIS lacks. Re-identification: the gap between geometry+input (0.6248) and geometry+appearance+input+fly (0.6910) is ~0.066 AP test, most of it from appearance; the fly-specific share is bounded by +0.006 today.

## Где обязано помогать
Must help: dark/night clips (fly night dip and DIS degradation), glare clips, and occlusion re-acquisition (lost-track stratum q_match AP 0.386). No-harm checks: (a) bright/day clips in every head (fly over DIS -0.0002 bright today); (b) Sintel validation EPE within the original 50-model ensemble range; (c) fly-likeness metrics (DSI and FRI correlations with experiments, ON/OFF polarity of the 31 characterised types) not below the 10th percentile of the original ensemble; (d) shift check on held-out BDD videos from other cities/weather (val7 and the 70 held-out videos).

## Отрицательный контроль
Same training recipe and budget applied to: (1) frozen fly with only front-end + decoder trained (stage 0) - isolates the value of training the fly itself; (2) degree- and sign-preserving rewired connectome initialised identically and trained identically (Dhiman-strength null; >= 3 rewiring seeds) - isolates topology; (3) parameter-matched non-biological recurrent network on the same hex input (same number of trainable parameters as the trained fly subset, same decoder) - isolates 'any small recurrent filter'; (4) DIS or RAFT on the same retina-normalised frames - the external motion baseline; (5) same fine-tune with the Sintel-only loss continued for the same steps (training-length control). Evaluation of every arm with the F16-calibrated score and illumination-scalar control in the heads.

## Вклад ветви
Log per checkpoint: per-group parameter displacement from the Sintel solution (|delta tau|, |delta V_rest|, |delta scale| by type, relative to the across-ensemble SD of that parameter), gradient-norm ratios between groups, mean activity and saturation per type, Sintel EPE, DSI/FRI correlation to known, and the downstream head AP with the fly features frozen at that checkpoint. A useful fine-tune shows moved parameters concentrated in photoreceptor/lamina types at night with T4/T5 tuning preserved; uniform drift of all 604 scales with falling DSI correlation means the model left the fly solution.

## Минимальный эффект и стоимость
Training cost: the original recipe is 250 000 iterations of batch 4 on 19-frame clips; on an RTX 5070 the project measured ~1.2 ms per network step at 1/16 density and 15.8 ms at full density (plan 7.5), so 19 frames x 4 clips x 250 000 iterations at full density is about 19 x 15.8 ms x 250 000 ~ 21 h forward-only, roughly 2-3x with backward through time, i.e. ~2-3 GPU-days for a from-scratch model [uncertain: backward cost not measured]. A fine-tune of 10 000-20 000 iterations is ~2-6 GPU-hours, consistent with the plan's 3-5 h estimate for K1. Controls multiply cost: stage 0 + stage 1 + rewired + parameter-matched RNN, each with 3 seeds = 12 runs x ~4 h ~ 48 GPU-hours (about 11 twelve-epoch detector runs in the project's currency of 4.3 h). MDE: head-level paired video bootstrap CI half-width ~0.004-0.006 AP (reliability head dark: [-0.0013; +0.0078]), so MDE ~0.008-0.01 AP; motion-error MDE on BDD clips can be computed per video and is small relative to the 52.77 -> 4.87 px gap, so the motion endpoint will be decisive quickly; tracker HOTA MDE on the 7-video bench is ~0.3-0.5 HOTA [uncertain], larger than the expected effect.

## Вердикт для проекта
Primary for the user's goal 'improve the fly further', secondary as an explanation of past failures. The frozen Sintel fly was never trained for dark, compressed, road-scale 30 Hz video or for object identity; the input-adaptation result (retina-lamina helps only the fly) is the strongest measured sign that training the fly's adaptation (time constants, resting potentials, front-end) on BDD can unlock signal. But the tracker-level ceiling is small (DetA does not move through association; stitching cues beyond geometry are non-significant), and task error is a weak guide to fly-likeness (Karaneen et al. 2026). Priority: high for the cheap diagnostics (decoder-only oracle, probe, gradient/Fisher check) and for stage 0/stage 1 with fly-likeness gates and the rewired-and-retrained control; medium for full 734-parameter training; low for pushing a contrastive reid loss through the fly before the readout ceiling is shown to be larger than +0.006 AP.
