# F13 Неустойчивость модели мухи между параметризациями: ансамбль FlyVis и разброс

↑ [[07 Исследования/Фундаментальные проблемы/Фундаментальные проблемы|Фундаментальные проблемы]]

> Lappalainen 2024 (ансамбль из 50 моделей), Nat. Neurosci. 2025: одна связность — много наборов параметров; прогон нескольких членов ансамбля; разброс как источник ложных выводов.

## Механизм
A connectome fixes WHO is wired to WHOM (and, via transcriptomics, the sign), but not the single-neuron and single-synapse parameters (time constants, resting potentials, synapse strength scalars). Lappalainen et al. (Nature 2024) therefore did not train one model: they trained an ensemble of 50 deep mechanistic networks (DMNs) with the same connectome and the same optic-flow task (Sintel), differing only in random initialization, because 'connectome and task-constraints might not uniquely constrain all model parameters'. Each member is a different local optimum. The ensemble MEDIAN is what predicts biology well (e.g. ON/OFF contrast preference correct for 31 of 31 cell types; T4 = ON-motion, T5 = OFF-motion), and better task performance correlates with better prediction of T4/T5 direction selectivity (p = 5.6e-6, Wald test). But individual members split into qualitatively different solutions: UMAP of T4c responses gives three clusters (upward-tuned, downward-tuned and non-direction-selective) with almost identical task error (5.297 / 5.316 / 5.357); TmY3 splits into four clusters. So at the level of a single member, a given cell type can have the 'wrong' tuning while the network still solves the task equally well. Beiran & Litwin-Kumar (Nature Neuroscience 2025) give the theory: a connectome-constrained 'student' trained to match a 'teacher' has a degenerate solution space; connectivity alone generally does not determine activity, and degeneracy is broken only by adding recordings of some neurons (or other constraints). This is the classic neuroscience degeneracy result (Prinz, Bucher & Marder 2004: similar network activity from disparate circuit parameters in a model stomatogastric ganglion). The ML twin of this is underspecification (D'Amour et al., JMLR 2022): many predictors with equal in-distribution validation performance behave differently under shift; the choice among them is made by arbitrary factors (seed) and is invisible on the validation set. Consequence for any downstream use of FlyVis: a feature read from ONE member (here flow/0000/000) is a sample from a distribution of solutions; a conclusion such as 'fly features add +0.006 AP over the eye input' or 'the shuffled connectome is −0.044 AP worse' is conditional on that member, and the between-member variance is an unmeasured error term — it can produce both false positives (a lucky member) and false negatives (a member whose relevant cell types, e.g. T4/T5 or lobula inputs, sit in a 'non-selective' cluster). Since downstream readouts in the project use specific cell types (T4/T5 for motion, many types for the signature), cluster membership of those types in member 000 directly determines what the readout can see. Additionally, ranking by validation loss (task error on Sintel flow) is the ranking on the WRONG task for the project (night BDD100K, signature, detection): the best Sintel member need not be the best for the project's readouts — the same underspecification argument.

## Где видно у нас
1) All fly results in the project come from a single ensemble member: plan journal (§ Журнал, entry on fly_signature/connectome null) states the model parameters (45 669 neurons, 1 513 231 connections, 734 free parameters) were taken from checkpoint flow/0000/000; plan registry item H12 'one ensemble member, uncalibrated decoder' and direction M5 '50-checkpoint ensemble and decoder calibration' were proposed on 18.09 but never run (no card in experiments/project_history.json mentions an ensemble). 2) All 50 members are already on disk (runs/flyvis/results/flow/0000/000…049). Reading validation_loss.h5 of each (done read-only during this research, not a catalog entry): Sintel validation loss of member 000 = 5.137 (the best; the ensemble is sorted by this loss), median 5.300, worst 5.678, SD 0.074 — i.e. member 000 is the Sintel-best, but the project never checked whether it is the best for its own tasks. 3) Results whose interpretation depends on the member: fly over eye input +0.006 AP [+0.001; +0.012] ([[fly_matched_controls_v1]], test); fly alone vs input −0.052 [−0.077; −0.030]; real vs shuffled connectome +0.044 [+0.027; +0.060] for geometry+fly but +0.0032 [−0.0028; +0.0100] on top of input ([[fly_connectome_null_v1]]); retina-lamina fixes the night drop of the fly +0.016 [+0.004; +0.029] ([[flyvis_retina_stageB_v1]]); fly as motion source 52.77 px error vs 4.87 px for DIS; the tracking head with fly-lamina ΔHOTA −0.014 [−0.037; +0.016] ([[e2e_motion_source_v1]]). The bootstrap CIs in these cards are over VIDEOS only — they do not contain the between-member term, so they understate the total uncertainty of any claim 'about FlyVis' (as opposed to 'about member 000'). 4) The shuffled-connectome null was also run with one member and one shuffle; a single realisation of the null plus a single realisation of the real model is a comparison of two draws, each with unknown spread. 5) Plan rule 6.4 'one seed is a filter, not a result' is honoured for YOLO, but the fly's own 'seed' (ensemble member) has never been varied — the rule is effectively violated for the subject of the thesis. 6) External supervisor experiment (StripNet + FlyWire branch, plan journal): strong base −0.65 pp, weak base +1.05 pp, one seed, epoch-to-epoch spread ±1 pp — the same pattern of single-realisation conclusions.

## Решения из литературы
(a) Treat the ensemble as the model, not a member. Lappalainen et al. draw all biological conclusions from the ensemble (median over members, or the top-10 by task error), and report cluster structure instead of a single tuning. The direct analogue: report every fly result as a distribution over K members (K = 5–10 of the 50, chosen before looking at project metrics, e.g. the top-10 by Sintel loss, or stratified by UMAP cluster of the cell types used). (b) Break degeneracy with extra constraints (Beiran & Litwin-Kumar 2025): adding activity data for a subset of neurons makes the remaining activity predictable. The project-side analogue is adding the project's own task as a constraint — fine-tuning the 734 parameters on BDD100K (candidate K1/K2 in plan [[05 План/7.6. Отбор следующих экспериментов по схеме RPM, 03.10|§7.6]]) — which selects among the degenerate solutions those useful for the project; but fine-tuning must itself start from several members, otherwise the result is again member-specific. (c) Ensembling for performance: deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan 2017) give consistent accuracy/calibration gains over single networks (typically a few tenths to ~1–2 points of accuracy on ImageNet-scale tasks [uncertain]) and model soups (Wortsman 2022: ViT-G/14 soup 90.94% ImageNet top-1, best single fine-tuned model lower) — averaging features or predictions across members reduces member-specific variance; weight averaging is NOT applicable across independently initialized FlyVis members (different basins), only averaging of outputs/features is. (d) Underspecification remedies (D'Amour 2022): stress-test the candidate predictors on the target shift (here: night/glare/occlusion subsets) and pick or weight members by that, instead of by in-distribution validation; but selection must be done on dev, reported on test, to avoid selection bias. (e) Statistical accounting (Bouthillier 2021): randomize every source of variance (here: ensemble member, connectome shuffle, decoder seed, video bootstrap) and use the probability of outperforming P(A>B) ≥ 0.75 over paired draws rather than a single-pair comparison; their simulation shows single-point comparisons give ≈10% false positives and ≈75% false negatives.

## Что сделать с мухой
1) Make 'member' an explicit experimental factor in all fly pipelines: the cache builders that currently load flow/0000/000 should take a member id; rebuild the fly feature caches for K members (proposed K = 5: 000, 001, 002 plus two members from different T4/T5 UMAP clusters; choose before seeing any project metric). Nothing in YOLO or the tracker changes; FlyVis stays frozen for this step. 2) Re-run the three decisive frozen-fly readouts per member with the same heads and splits: (i) fly over eye input ([[fly_matched_controls_v1]] protocol, AP of pairs), (ii) real vs shuffled connectome — with one shuffle per member, so the null is also a distribution ([[fly_connectome_null_v1]] protocol), (iii) retina-lamina night effect ([[flyvis_retina_stageB_v1]] protocol). Report each as mean over members with a two-level bootstrap (resample members, then videos). 3) Ensemble-of-members feature: concatenate or average the readout features (or head outputs) of K members — the cheapest possible 'improve the fly' that requires no training of the fly; it tells whether the +0.006 AP over input is limited by member noise. 4) For the planned fine-tuning of the fly (K1: self-supervised on BDD100K video; K2: same/different object), start from at least 3 different members and keep the 734 parameters trainable, decoder/readout trainable, retina-lamina in front; YOLO frozen. If the three fine-tuned members converge to similar project performance, fine-tuning has broken the degeneracy in the project's favour (the Beiran & Litwin-Kumar mechanism); if they diverge, the claim must be stated as member-dependent. 5) Use the ensemble's own diversity diagnostics (flyvis EnsembleView, cluster analysis of T4/T5 and lobula-input types) to label each member; this lets the thesis say WHICH biological solution helps (e.g. 'members with direction-selective T4c help the motion readout') — a fly-specific statement that no generic CNN can make.

## Тест до постройки
Before any new fly module: (1) Member-spread probe on existing caches. Take the existing frozen-fly signature pipeline (AP of same/different-object pairs on dev/test, the [[fly_matched_controls_v1]] setup) and recompute only the fly features for 5 members; train the same light head; compute per member the Δ(input+fly − input) and Δ(fly − input). Decision rule written in advance: if the between-member SD of Δ is ≥ the half-width of the video-bootstrap CI (~0.005 AP for input+fly − input), member noise dominates and every single-member fly conclusion in the catalog must be re-labelled 'member 000 only'; if SD is ≪ CI, member choice is irrelevant and the H12/M5 hypothesis is closed. (2) Correlation check: Spearman between Sintel validation loss (5.137…5.678 over the 50 members, already on disk) and the project metric across the 5–10 members; if ρ ≈ 0, Sintel-best is not project-best and selection must be done on dev. (3) CKA/linear-CKA between the readout features of members on the same project clips: low CKA for the cell types used (T4/T5, lobula inputs) predicts large downstream spread before any head is trained. (4) Oracle-of-members: max over members of the dev metric (with test reported) bounds what member selection can give; if even the best member is within the CI of member 000, ensemble/selection is not a lever.

## Потолок
Member selection or ensembling cannot exceed what the fly representation family carries. Measured ceilings in the project: fly signature on top of input +0.006 AP (≈1% of 0.625 AP), and appearance+fly on top of geometry stitching are not significant in HOTA (+0.08 HOTA, CI through zero; geometry stitching itself +0.79 HOTA over 58.47 = +1.4%). Thus even a perfect ensemble that doubled the fly's marginal AP would be expected to stay below ~+0.1 HOTA (<0.2% of baseline) on the tracker [uncertain]. For the motion role the gap is far larger (52.77 px vs 4.87 px for DIS) and is not plausibly explained by member choice (the whole ensemble was trained on Sintel flow with similar task errors, SD of validation loss 0.074 around median 5.30, i.e. ~1.4%), so member selection closes at most a few % of that gap [uncertain]. The real value of this item is epistemic: it converts claims about 'member 000' into claims about FlyVis, and removes a hidden variance term from every later comparison (including fine-tuning, K1/K2).

## Где обязано помогать
The member effect must be checked where the fly is claimed to matter: dark videos (the 27 dark dev+test videos used in [[flyvis_retina_stageB_v1]], where the retina-lamina +0.016 effect lives), and the identity-signature task on test/val7; secondarily the 30 Hz motion subset. No-harm check: on bright videos and on all-video aggregates the ensemble/selected member must not be worse than member 000 beyond the CI (e.g. bright-video AP within ±0.0015, the CI width observed for lamina on bright videos).

## Отрицательный контроль
(1) Shuffled connectome per member (degree- and sign-preserving shuffle; one shuffle per member, so real-vs-null is compared as paired distributions; [[fly_connectome_null_v1]] protocol). (2) Matched input control: the same eye input (721 facets) without the network, unchanged across members (it is member-independent, so it is the fixed reference). (3) Random-parameter member: same connectome, untrained (initial) parameters — tells whether Sintel training matters at all for the project readout. (4) Parameter-count-matched generic network on the same input. A gain can be credited to 'the fly' only if mean over members beats (1) and (2) with a two-level bootstrap CI excluding zero, and not merely if member 000 does.

## Вклад ветви
For the frozen-fly heads: report per member the head weight norm on fly features vs input features and the drop when fly features are zeroed at inference (ablation). For fine-tuning from several members: track per member the change of the 734 parameters (L2 distance from init, per parameter group: time constants, resting potentials, synapse scalars) per epoch, the ratio of gradient norms fly-parameters/decoder, and whether cluster labels of key cell types (T4/T5 direction selectivity index on the flyvis moving-edge stimulus) change during fine-tuning — this shows whether fine-tuning moves members between biological solutions.

## Минимальный эффект и стоимость
Cost: the fly feature caches for one member are the expensive step; the plan estimated ~3 h GPU for the shuffled-connectome control (one extra fly pass over the caches), so 5 members ≈ 15 h GPU on the RTX 5070 [uncertain], heads are CPU/minutes. Fine-tuning (K1 pilot ~1–2 h GPU per run) from 3 members ≈ 3–6 h. MDE: with a video-bootstrap CI half-width of ≈0.005 AP for (input+fly − input) (test CI [+0.0007; +0.0118]), a single comparison has MDE ≈ 0.007 AP (80% power, α = 0.05). Adding K members adds the between-member variance σ_m²/K; if σ_m ≈ 0.005 (unknown, to be measured [uncertain]), K = 5 keeps the total MDE ≈ 0.008 AP, while a single member hides σ_m entirely. For the shuffled-null difference (+0.044 alone; +0.003 on top of input) 5 member-pairs give a paired test whose MDE is roughly σ_pair·2.8/√5 ≈ 1.25·σ_pair. Rule: measure σ_m on 3 members first (≈9 h GPU), then decide K by n = (2.8·σ_m/δ)² for the effect δ of interest.

## Вердикт для проекта
Secondary as an explanation of failures, primary as a methodological gap. Member choice is very unlikely to explain the big negatives (fly motion 52.77 vs 4.87 px; DetA unchanged; detector-side fly front-ends −2…−15 pp) — those are effects of task/domain mismatch far larger than the ensemble spread on Sintel (~1.4% SD of validation loss). But every small positive or null fly result (+0.006 AP over input, +0.003 over input vs shuffled, +0.016 night with lamina) rests on one member and one null realisation, so the thesis cannot currently say 'FlyVis does X', only 'member flow/0000/000 does X'. Priority: high and cheap relative to its value — run the 3–5-member probe (H12/M5) BEFORE fine-tuning the fly (K1/K2), and start any fine-tuning from ≥3 members. Novelty doubt (brief): ensemble reporting is standard practice in the FlyVis paper itself; its use here is methodological, not a novelty item.
