# 6.2.3.4. D15 Сильная база и честное сравнение YOLO26 против RT-DETR, D-FINE, DEIM, RF-DETR и детекторов лучших трекеров BDD100K

> Что сейчас лучшее по детекции на BDD100K (изображения и MOT) в реальном времени: YOLO26, YOLO11, RT-DETRv2, D-FINE, DEIM, RF-DETR; детекторы лучших трекеров BDD100K MOT (DetA); как честно заявить «лучше YOLO26»: равный бюджет, сиды, разрешение, контроль того же входа; сколько дают простые рычаги (960 при обучении, модель m).

## Суть
On COCO, the real-time detectors that are strongest at small sizes are now DETR-type models with pretrained backbones: RF-DETR-S 53.0 AP, DEIMv2-S 50.9, D-FINE-S 50.7 with Objects365 pretraining, RT-DETRv4-S 49.7, DEIM-D-FINE-S about 49.0. YOLO26s scores 48.6 (47.8 in end-to-end mode). One size up costs YOLO26 3.3x the FLOPs for +4.5 AP (YOLO26m 53.1). On BDD100K, no published head-to-head comparison of these real-time models exists, either on the images or on MOT. The top BDD100K MOT trackers (ByteTrack, GHOST, GeneralTrack) all reuse the same public YOLOX detector, and they report mHOTA/HOTA but not DetA. So 'better than YOLO26' can only be shown in-house, by a controlled comparison. It needs: the same data, epochs (or GPU-hours) and train/test resolution; at least 3 paired seeds with a CI on the difference; a same-input control without the fly; and checking that the gain survives the cheap levers, namely training at 960 and the m size. Any fly gain must be measured on top of those levers or at equal compute, because the levers are likely larger than the fly effect.

## Место мухи
This item decides the yardstick, not the fly mechanism. It fixes how any fly variant (D13 rescoring or distillation, D14 fly-gated weighting, an input channel) must be compared. (1) The baseline is not 'YOLO26s at 640, 12 epochs, 1 seed'. It is the best of {YOLO26s trained at 960; YOLO26m at 640} at the same epoch budget, or YOLO26s at equal GPU-hours. A fly detector that beats YOLO26s-640 but not YOLO26s-960 has not shown that the fly helps. (2) The fly arm must have a same-input twin. If the fly adds input channels or a teacher, the twin gets the same pixels and temporal context without the connectome: receptor-only, DoG/wavelet front-end, shuffled connectome. This is the detection version of the project's 'same input for DIS' control. (3) Where the fly can most credibly win is on the subsets that define the problem: night threshold losses, age-0 recall, night-occluded objects, plus DetA/FN through the frozen tracker. Overall COCO-style mAP is not where it is likely to win. The claim should be pre-registered on those subsets. (4) DETR alternatives (D-FINE, DEIM, RF-DETR) are a separate question: they give a stronger detector, but they change many factors at once (backbone pretraining, matching, loss, resolution). They can be reported as context, not as a single-factor comparison to the fly. A fly add-on can also be ported to them; the RT-DETRv4 distillation interface (a cosine loss on an encoder feature) is the natural place.

## Какую потерю закрывает
Indirect, through choice of the base. Training at 960 should mainly help small objects (56.7% found) and some BLINDNESS: tiny objects that never become candidates at the 640 training scale. FixRes shows that testing above the training resolution shifts apparent object size away from the training distribution, and the project trains at 640 but runs at 960. The m size mainly adds capacity, which could help occlusion and cluttered background. Neither lever specifically addresses the night THRESHOLD deficit or the age-0 gap. That is where the fly has room to add value once the stronger base is in place, and the comparison should be designed to show exactly that: a gain on night threshold and age 0 that persists on top of the 960/m levers.

## Пересечение с чужими работами
Benchmarks and protocols are not novelty, but they define cannot_claim. Several claims are ruled out. 'Better than YOLO26' must not rest on a single seed, on unequal epochs or resolutions, or on comparing against COCO-reported numbers. RF-DETR reports YOLO11-S at 44.1 AP under its own protocol versus 47.0 in Ultralytics' table, which shows that protocol alone moves numbers by about 3 AP. 'State of the art on BDD100K MOT' is not possible: the project's HOTA 59.27 / DetA 58.17 come from its own val subset and class protocol, while the published BDD100K numbers are mHOTA/mTETA on the full val (ByteTrack HOTA 61.3, GeneralTrack 63.1, mHOTA 45.3 / 46.9). Our numbers are not comparable without rerunning the official evaluation. No published BDD100K DetA reference was found for top trackers. Allowed: 'under a controlled protocol on BDD100K MOT, the fly variant improves night threshold recall / age-0 recall / DetA over YOLO26s with the same training budget, resolution and seeds'.

## Проверка против сильной базы
Cross-family comparisons in the papers above are mostly COCO-only. Their training budgets differ: Objects365 pretraining, 72-500 epochs, and different resolutions (RF-DETR uses 384-704 px per size). So they are not equal-budget comparisons, and RF-DETR documents latency and export pitfalls. None is reproduced on BDD100K MOT by an independent party. The BDD100K MOT tracker comparisons are fair in one sense: they share one YOLOX detector, so the differences are association only. That also means there is no published evidence of which modern real-time detector is best for BDD100K MOT DetA. Implication: the project must build its own strong baseline. The project's numbers already show the noise floor: seed spread 0.0144 mAP at 12 epochs, night mAP50 control 0.3772 +/- 0.0031, and DRB's sign flip between 12 and 100 epochs. Bouthillier et al. 2021 show that ignoring sources of variance (seed, data order, hyperparameter tuning) routinely produces false rankings, and they recommend multiple seeds with randomized data order and a test on the paired difference.

## Разрез по условиям
COCO papers give only APs/APm/APl; DEIMv2 and RF-DETR highlight small-object AP. BDD100K detection model zoo: overall AP only. BDD100K images carry weather and time-of-day attributes, so night subsets can be built in-house. Trackers on BDD100K: per-class mean metrics (mHOTA, mTETA), no night or first-appearance breakdown. No paper found compares YOLO26 vs DETR-type real-time detectors on BDD100K night. The only reliable night baseline is the project's own: night mAP50 0.3772 +/- 0.0031, and the threshold, blindness and age decompositions.

## Дешёвая проверка
Gate D15-A, resolution curve without training (about 1-2 h): run best.pt on val at test sizes 640, 800, 960, 1120 and 1280 using the existing evaluation, and record overall and night mAP50, small-object recall, threshold-lost and blind fractions, and DetA through the frozen tracker on val7. This shows how much of the 960 benefit is already reached by test-time upscaling and whether larger test sizes keep helping, which predicts the value of training at 960. Gate D15-B, noise floor of the fine-tune protocol (about 3.5 h): fine-tune best.pt for 3 epochs at 640 with 3 seeds and the same settings, and measure the spread of night mAP50 and age-0 recall. This sets the minimum detectable effect for all cheap fly experiments (D13/D14 fine-tunes). Gate D15-C, cheap lever estimates (about 7-9 h): (i) fine-tune best.pt for 3 epochs at imgsz 960, 2 seeds [uncertain: about 2.25x the per-epoch cost, about 48 min/epoch]; (ii) YOLO26m fine-tuned from COCO weights, 3 epochs at 640, 2 seeds. Compare each against D15-B at equal epochs. Pre-registered decision: if a lever raises night mAP50 by more than 2x the D15-B spread, it becomes the base for all fly arms. Pre-registered claim rule for the final 'better than YOLO26': at least 3 (target 5) paired seeds per arm, equal epochs and resolution, a 95% bootstrap CI on the paired difference of night mAP50 and of age-0 recall at equal birth precision that excludes 0, no day mAP50 loss beyond the control's SD, DetA/FN through the frozen tracker on val7/test, and a same-input no-fly twin that does not reproduce the gain. 12-epoch results are screening only; the claim requires 100-epoch confirmation of the winning arm against the control.

## Стоимость у нас
Training cost on the RTX 5070 12 GB: YOLO26s at 640 takes about 21.5 min/epoch (12 epochs about 4.3 h; 100 epochs about 35 h). At 960 the cost per epoch should rise about 2.25x by pixel count [uncertain: 12 epochs about 9.5-10 h, batch size may need halving]. YOLO26m costs about 3.3x the FLOPs [uncertain: 12 epochs about 10-13 h at 640]. A fair 3-seed comparison of control vs fly at 12 epochs is 6 runs, about 26 h at 640 or about 60 h at 960. A 100-epoch confirmation is about 35 h per run at 640. DETR alternatives (D-FINE-S / DEIM-S / RF-DETR-S) need a separate code base, conversion of BDD100K MOT to COCO JSON, and their own schedules. Their per-epoch cost on a 12 GB card is higher than YOLO26s [uncertain: 1.5-3x], and they normally train 72+ epochs, so one fair DETR baseline is about 2-4 days of GPU time. This is reasonable as a one-off context point, not as part of every ablation. Inference: YOLO26s 2.5 ms vs YOLO26m 4.7 ms on T4 at 640; at 960 roughly 2.25x more [uncertain].

## Ожидаемый эффект
Cheap levers: training at 960 vs 640 with testing at 960 should help small and distant objects. Expected gain is a few points of mAP50 on BDD100K, larger on night small objects [uncertain: +1 to +4 mAP50 overall]. The m size on COCO adds +4.5 AP over s; on BDD100K it is plausibly +2 to +4 mAP50-95 [uncertain]. Both are likely larger than any fly-side effect at 12 epochs, where D13 and D14 priors are below the 0.0144 seed spread overall. Therefore the expected honest outcome is not 'fly beats YOLO26m-960 on overall mAP'. It is 'on top of the best lever, the fly adds X points of night threshold or age-0 recall with a CI excluding 0', or a documented null. Tracker level: DetA moved by 0.04 under all tracker-side changes (58.21 -> 58.17), so any detector lever that cuts FN 15 763 is the first that can move DetA. A +2 mAP50 lever could plausibly move DetA by +0.5-1.5 [uncertain]. For DETR alternatives, COCO margins over YOLO26s at the S size are +1.1 to +4.4 AP (DEIM-S to RF-DETR-S). Whether this transfers to BDD100K night at 12-epoch budgets is unknown, and DETRs usually need longer schedules.

## Риски
1) Unequal comparisons: different epochs, resolutions, pretraining (COCO vs Objects365 vs DINOv2/v3 backbones), augmentation, or export precision. RF-DETR shows protocol differences of about 3 AP for YOLO11-S and FP16 export collapse for D-FINE. 2) Seed noise: 0.0144 mAP at 12 epochs; a single-seed 'win' is not evidence. 3) Sign flip between 12 and 100 epochs (DRB): fast-converging variants look better on short schedules. DEIM is explicitly designed to win at short schedules, so short-budget comparisons favor it. 4) Operating point: comparing at a fixed conf threshold across models with different calibration is unfair. Use AP, recall at equal precision, or equal birth precision for tracking. 5) Test-resolution mismatch: training at 640 and testing at 960 already confounds the baseline; state both sizes explicitly. 6) Leaderboard comparability: the project's HOTA/DetA protocol (subset, classes, 5 Hz labels) differs from the official BDD100K mHOTA/TETA, so do not place the project in published tables without rerunning the official evaluator. 7) Compute fairness: a fly front-end that costs inference time must be compared with a bigger YOLO at equal latency (the DRB +17% time lesson). 8) Licenses: Ultralytics YOLO26 AGPL-3.0, so distributing derived code or weights carries AGPL obligations; D-FINE, DEIM, RT-DETR and RF-DETR N-L are Apache-2.0; RF-DETR XL/2XL are PML 1.0; BDD100K is for non-commercial research only.
