# 6.3.1.8. A6 Внешность ночью и при пересвете что ломается и как чинят

## Суть
At night, appearance cues for association degrade in four ways: sensor noise swamps shallow features, deep features respond weakly to objects, colour becomes unreliable under artificial lighting, and headlights create glare and blooming. Under overexposure, clipped pixels carry no texture at all, so that information is physically lost. Work since 2024 fixes this in four ways: (a) train for degradation-invariant features instead of enhancing images first (LTrack); (b) suppress glare with a mask of saturated pixels (DNDM vehicle re-ID); (c) distil illumination-invariant features from paired or enhanced images (nighttime person re-ID); (d) lean on motion and geometry when appearance fails. The fly-side analogue is already in the project: photoreceptor adaptation plus lamina contrast normalisation in front of FlyVis. Two cheap transfers remain: masking saturated facets in the fly input, and illumination-normalised variants of the stored appearance controls.

## Относимость к проекту
This is registry N9 (fly input out of distribution at night) plus the night and overexposure conditions in the thesis title. The project already has three results here. (1) The lamina front end removed the fly's night drop in the reliability head: lamina minus no-retina on dark videos +0.009 AP [+0.005; +0.014], and 0.000 on bright ones (runs/motion_reliability_lamina/strata_bootstrap.json). (2) Appearance weakens at night but does not collapse. In re-ID pairs, (geometry + thumbnail/HSV) - geometry is +0.162 AP [+0.100; +0.218] on dark videos vs +0.201 [+0.166; +0.235] on bright ones (runs/fly_reid_controls/strata_bootstrap.json). By BDD timeofday (computed now from the per-video AP in the same report, dev+test), it is night +0.155 (n=29) vs day +0.212 (n=37); the night-minus-day difference is -0.057 [-0.123; +0.008]. (3) The fly's increment over its input is larger in the dark (+0.0102 vs +0.0054), but the interaction is not significant (night - day +0.004 [-0.003; +0.012]). Overexposure has not been tested for the fly at all: the 30-video overexposed subset comes from the val split, not from the videos used for the signatures.

## Пересечение с чужими работами
Low-light MOT (LMOT/LTrack, CRTrack), day-night vehicle re-ID with glare suppression (DNDM) and nighttime person re-ID (NightReID, IDF, CENet) already cover 'appearance at night' on the camera side. Biologically inspired adaptive front ends (photoreceptor adaptation, Naka-Rushton, divisive or contrast normalisation) are classical and are also used in event and neuromorphic vision. cannot_claim: glare masking, illumination-invariant re-ID, or a retina-like front end as contributions; also not 'first overexposure study' (novelty is not argued from absent publications). [uncertain] No work was found that feeds a connectome-constrained FlyVis eye through an adaptive front end for night tracking; that is at most an application-level detail. The measured night benefit of the lamina for the fly is a project result, not a method novelty.

## Результаты у авторов
LMOT/LTrack (CVPR 2024): 32 RAW videos, 35,120 frames, 815,550 boxes, captured by a dual camera with aligned well-lit and low-light pairs; static or slowly moving urban cameras [uncertain]. With ByteTrack on LMOT: trained on low-light only, HOTA 28.0; well-lit only, 13.5; all, 28.1; LTrack, 29.4 (+1.3). Enhancement front ends: LLFlow 28.8 at about 11x the FLOPs, SDSD 29.1, DNF 27.6, SMOID 28.0. On LMOT-real with OC-SORT, all 34.6 vs LTrack 35.1. A detector trained on well-lit images drops from 37.0 mAP (well-lit test) to 3.1 mAP (low-light test). With ground-truth boxes (LMOT val), appearance-only association falls from HOTA 87.3 / AssA 77.1 / IDF1 82.5 in well-lit to 53.8 / 32.1 / 40.0 in low light, while motion + appearance reaches 83.4 / 77.5 / 85.0 in low light. [uncertain] This row mapping is read from extracted PDF text. The authors' conclusion: appearance is effective in well-lit scenes but not in low light. DNDM (CVPR 2024): DN-Wild has 2,286 identities, 85,945 day and 54,952 night images; on the balanced DN-348, Rank-1 70.7% and mAP 47.5%. Glare suppression prompts the network with a binary mask of high-intensity pixels. [uncertain] Per-module ablation numbers were not retrieved. Into the Fog (MOT17 with synthetic fog): the HOTA drop at fog level 3 is 25% for ByteTrack and 48% for FairMOT (a joint re-ID model); detection is the main bottleneck. NightReID (AAAI 2025): 1,500 identities, more than 53,000 images; daytime re-ID methods degrade strongly at night. [uncertain] Exact numbers were not retrieved.

## Уровень измерения
LMOT/CRTrack/Into the Fog: HOTA/AssA/IDF1 after tracking. DNDM/NightReID/IDF: retrieval mAP and Rank-1 on cropped identities, not tracking. Project: pair AP for re-ID, and AP of the reliability head for motion; HOTA only for stitching (appearance +0.08 HOTA over geometry, CI crosses 0).

## Согласованный контроль
Authors: LMOT uses paired well-lit/low-light videos of the same scenes, the strongest same-content control in the literature; it compares enhancement front ends against training on low-light data at similar parameter count. DNDM compares day and night domains of the same identities. None uses a same-information control in the project's sense. Project: the lamina test has a luminance-matched control and a shuffled-fly control. With the lamina, the fly vs shuffled on top of DIS on dark videos is +0.0046 [+0.0011; +0.0082]. For appearance at night, the controls are the raw 721-facet input (same field of view) and the thumbnail/HSV descriptors, all stored in runs/fly_signatures.

## Разрез по условиям
LMOT: low-light vs well-lit (paired) and synthetic vs real night. DNDM: day-day, night-night and day-night retrieval. Into the Fog: 4 fog levels, homogeneous vs heterogeneous. Overexposure: only DNDM (headlight glare) is close; no MOT work with an overexposure split was found. Project: luminance dark/bright and BDD timeofday/weather/scene, all available now; an overexposure split for the fly videos is missing.

## Стоимость у нас
Test 1, CPU, minutes, uses the existing caches: illumination-normalised appearance controls from the stored vectors in runs/fly_signatures (first/last_thumb 16x16 grey, first/last_hist 40-bin HSV). Variants: per-crop z-normalised thumbnail (the lamina analogue); hue-saturation histogram without the 8 brightness bins; rank or quantile normalisation. Re-run the eval_fly_reid.py ladder and the dark/bright bootstrap. Test 2, CPU, minutes: saturated-pixel fraction on the 70 dev+test videos (threshold 250/255, as in scripts/build_overexposed_subset.py) to create an overexposure stratum for the existing per-video AP. Test 3, GPU: a glare/saturation mask in the retina front end (clipped facets set to the local adapted mean before the fly). This needs new signatures: [uncertain] about 1.5-2 h wall time on 70 dev+test videos with 7 processes, estimated from 390 s per 1213-frame video in runs/fly_signatures/*/signatures.json. Long runs only through Start-Process at Idle priority.

## Ожидаемый эффект
Test 1: normalising the thumbnail should recover part of the night gap of appearance over geometry (+0.155 night vs +0.212 day), perhaps a few thousandths of AP at night with a small loss in daytime. [uncertain] This magnitude follows from the lamina result (+0.009 AP for the fly in the dark), not from a direct measurement. Test 2: expect few strongly overexposed videos among the 70 dev+test videos (37 day, 4 dawn/dusk), so this is likely a small-n stratum [uncertain]. Test 3: effect expected to be at most the size of the lamina gain (+0.009 AP dark) and only on glare-heavy night and overexposed videos; the fly's aggregate increment over its input (+0.006 AP) is unlikely to change sign [uncertain]. HOTA: none of these is expected to move HOTA beyond its CI. Appearance on top of geometry gives only +0.08 HOTA in stitching, and LMOT shows that at night the robust cue is motion, which matches the project's finding that geometry-only stitching gives +0.79 HOTA.

## Риски
(1) Porting LTrack-style degradation-suppression training would mean retraining the detector or backbone, which is forbidden (YOLO26 is frozen). Only descriptor-side and front-end fixes are allowed. (2) An enhancement front end in front of the fly changes the fly's input and could inflate the 'fly over input' increment unless the input control is taken after the same front end. The project already compares against the post-lamina input; keep it that way. (3) Glare masks tuned by looking at test videos would be post-hoc; fix the threshold on train/dev. (4) Night and dark strata are confounded with city scenes and traffic density. (5) DNDM and NightReID numbers are for crop retrieval across cameras, not within-video re-ID at 30 Hz with a moving camera; do not transfer their magnitudes. (6) The overexposed val subset and the fly signature videos are different splits; do not mix them.

## Как подписать заимствование
Low-light MOT problem statement, paired dataset and the finding that appearance fails at night: Wang et al. 2024 (LMOT). Glare masking: Li et al. 2024 (DNDM). Illumination distillation: IDF/CENet/NightReID authors. Photoreceptor/lamina front end: biological models (Naka-Rushton adaptation, lamina contrast normalisation) combined with FlyVis (Lappalainen et al. 2024). Ours: applying these to a per-track FlyVis fovea on BDD100K, the same-information controls, the luminance and BDD-attribute strata, and the saturated-facet masking test.
