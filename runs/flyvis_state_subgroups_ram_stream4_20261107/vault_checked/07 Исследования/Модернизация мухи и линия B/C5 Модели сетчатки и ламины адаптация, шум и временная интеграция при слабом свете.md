# C5 Модели сетчатки и ламины: адаптация, шум и временная интеграция при слабом свете

↑ [[07 Исследования/Модернизация мухи и линия B/Модернизация мухи и линия B|Модернизация мухи и линия B]]

## Суть
Models of the first stages of fly vision that FlyVis does not contain: photoreceptor R1-R6 light adaptation (Weber / Naka-Rushton gain with an adapting half-saturation point, bandwidth that narrows in dim light), lamina LMC (L1-L3) centre-surround contrast normalisation, a separate luminance channel (L3), fast luminance gain control in the medulla (Tm1 divisive, Tm9 quadratic normalisation with Dm12 spatial pooling over about 8-20 degrees) and recurrent L1/L2 feedback that temporally sharpens intensity changes. In dim light such stages trade temporal and spatial resolution for signal-to-noise (neural summation). The project already has a simplified photoreceptor + lamina frontend in front of the fly (tracking/retina.py, stage B completed_positive); the question here is which missing pieces (light-dependent temporal integration, noise-aware gain, luminance channel, spatial pooling, highlight/glare handling) are worth adding and testing.

## Относимость к проекту
Directly H9 (fly input out of distribution at night) and the night / overexposure part of the thesis. Measured so far: on val7 the lamina raised rho(|fly flow|, prediction error) from 0.068 to 0.154 while global normalisation ('norm') gave 0.061, and in stage B the lamina fixed the night drop (dark videos, DIS+fly head, lamina minus no-retina +0.0094 [+0.0049; +0.0142]; bright +0.0003). Open issues this topic addresses: the fly still adds only +0.0028 [-0.0007; +0.0064] over DIS on dark videos; the dark-video rho criterion (> 0.1) on b1d22449 was not met (-0.011 photoreceptor, +0.044 lamina); floors (dark_floor, lamina_floor) are hand-set rather than derived from camera noise; there is no temporal integration that depends on light level and no explicit handling of headlight glare / clipped highlights (overexposure). Better night signatures could also help IDSw (re-ID: fly over input +0.0102 on dark videos).

## Пересечение с чужими работами
A biomimetic photoreceptor + LMC stage before a motion detector is exactly Brinkworth & O'Carroll 2009 (the project's retina.py already cites it); luminance gain control stages are characterised by Ketkar 2020/2023 and Gur 2024; bio-inspired preprocessing for night robustness in CV exists (Stracke et al. 2025 for segmentation on Dark Zurich/ACDC; Hung & Lin 2024 for detection on ExDark); learned low-pass / degradation suppression for low-light MOT exists (LTrack 2024). [uncertain] No work was found that puts such a frontend in front of FlyVis inside a tracker. cannot_claim: the frontend concept, physiological fidelity (our model has no adaptive cut-off frequency, no dynamic gamma, no photon-noise model, constants not fitted to R1-R6 data), and that the retina rather than simple range normalisation is the cause beyond what the 'norm' control already shows.

## Результаты у авторов
[uncertain] Brinkworth & O'Carroll 2009: adding the photoreceptor stage made optic-flow coding largely independent of contrast and illumination in natural scenes, with a reported improvement of more than 600% in their model (figure as quoted in the project's note, not re-verified). Ketkar 2023: L3-driven circuitry implements bidirectional luminance gain control at fast and slow timescales, preventing contrast underestimation in dim light and overestimation in bright light; photoreceptor adaptation alone is insufficient. Gur 2024: Tm1 (linear divisive normalisation) and Tm9 (quadratic normalisation, GluClalpha-dependent, pooled luminance from Dm12) are the first stages with rapid luminance-invariant contrast; optimal pooling radius 8-20 degrees, matching natural-scene luminance correlations; T4/T5 inherit this stability. Pang et al. 2024: voltage imaging plus a dynamical model show recurrent feedback onto L1/L2 sharpens intensity transitions and compensates motion blur on moving natural images. LTrack (CVPR 2024, low-light MOT, aligned bright/dark dual-camera video, 32 sequences, 35K frames): HOTA 35.1, AssA 38.9, IDF1 40.4, MOTA 45.2, DetA 30.7 on LMOT-real, with an adaptive low-pass downsample module and degradation-suppression learning. Hung & Lin 2024 (ExDark): mAP50 0.548 (COCO pre-trained) to 0.588 (retrained) to 0.603 (dark-adaptation fusion). IA-YOLO 2022: ExDark mAP 36.42 to 40.37 with a 165K-parameter CNN predicting enhancement-filter parameters. Frame rates and camera motion: biology uses controlled stimuli; LMOT is static/slow surveillance-like video [uncertain]; none uses 30 Hz dashcam driving video.

## Уровень измерения
Biology: neural responses (imaging, voltage imaging) and behaviour; Brinkworth: flow-coding invariance; CV: detection mAP, segmentation mIoU, MOT HOTA/IDF1 (LTrack). In the project: correlation rho and pair-level AP of the reliability head (stages A and B); end-to-end HOTA with the lamina fly in the tracker was 0 (e2e_motion_source_v1, dHOTA test -0.01 [-0.04; +0.02]).

## Согласованный контроль
Project: the 'norm' control (global normalisation to the same mean/range) gave no gain (rho 0.061 vs 0.068 none), so the effect is not only about range; a shuffled-connectome fly with the lamina was also measured (dark videos DIS+fly lamina 0.5242 vs shuffled 0.5197, difference +0.0046 [+0.0011; +0.0082]). [uncertain] No record was found in the plan of the same frontend fed to DIS (planned in M1 as the paired control), so it is not known whether the frontend helps any motion estimator equally. Literature: Brinkworth compares with/without the photoreceptor stage; Ketkar/Gur use genetic silencing (L3, Tm9, GluClalpha) as causal controls; the CV papers have no same-information control.

## Разрез по условиям
Project: dark (< 60/255, 27 dev+test videos) vs bright (43) with per-video bootstrap; no overexposure/glare or occlusion split. Literature: biology varies luminance over decades; Stracke evaluates Dark Zurich/ACDC night; LMOT is low-light only; no work splits by headlight glare.

## Стоимость у нас
Cheap and CPU-heavy. Extensions of tracking/retina.py (light-dependent temporal low-pass tau_filter as a function of local adaptation level; floors derived from a Poisson-Gaussian camera-noise estimate; Dm12-like pooling for the gain signal; a highlight-compression branch for glare; an L3-like luminance channel exported as an extra readout feature, since FlyVis takes one R1-R6 channel). Each variant needs recomputation of fovea outputs because the fly input changes: val7 first (7 videos, fast lamina 67 ms/frame, minutes to an hour), then the 27 dark + 43 bright dev/test videos and the train videos for the heads; the plan's estimate for this class of run is about 1-1.5 GPU hours per frontend variant. Existing caches (runs/train_cache_retina_lamina4, runs/flyvis_fovea_flow_retina_*) serve as the lamina reference; heads and bootstrap re-use scripts already in use (motion reliability ladder, scripts/eval_fly_reid.py).

## Ожидаемый эффект
[uncertain] Small increments on top of the lamina: the main night failure was already removed (+0.0094 AP on dark videos) and the fly over DIS on dark videos is +0.0028 (CI touches 0). A light-dependent temporal integration and noise-aware floors may add a few thousandths of AP on dark pairs and could push the dark-video fly-over-DIS CI above zero; the largest untested headroom is overexposure / glare scenes, which have never been split. No HOTA gain is expected given the end-to-end result.

## Риски
(1) Tuning the frontend on test videos would leak; tune on train/val7 only and report dev+test once. (2) Temporal low-pass adds lag that hurts motion estimation at 30 Hz; dim-light summation is a trade-off, not a free gain. (3) Floors can amplify H.264 compression noise in BDD100K night frames. (4) Clipped highlights carry no information; no frontend can recover saturated pixels. (5) Several frontend changes at once break the one-condition rule; add one mechanism per variant. (6) Without feeding the same frontend to DIS the gain cannot be attributed to the fly. (7) Effects are within bootstrap noise at the video level (27 dark videos).

## Как подписать заимствование
Borrowed: adaptive photoreceptor + LMC frontend idea (Brinkworth & O'Carroll 2009); luminance gain control and pooling scale (Ketkar et al. 2020, 2023; Gur et al. 2024); recurrent temporal sharpening (Pang et al. 2024); dim-light spatial/temporal summation (Warrant 1999); low-pass / degradation suppression for low-light MOT (Wang et al. 2024). Ours: the simplified implementation in tracking/retina.py, its placement in front of a per-track foveal FlyVis eye in an MOT tracker on BDD100K, the 'norm' and shuffled-connectome controls, and the dark/bright pair-level evaluation.
