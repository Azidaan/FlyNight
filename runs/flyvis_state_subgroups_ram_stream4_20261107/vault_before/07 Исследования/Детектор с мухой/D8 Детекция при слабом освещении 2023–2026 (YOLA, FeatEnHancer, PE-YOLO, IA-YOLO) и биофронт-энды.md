# D8 Детекция при слабом освещении 2023–2026 (YOLA, FeatEnHancer, PE-YOLO, IA-YOLO) и биофронт-энды

↑ [[07 Исследования/Детектор с мухой/Детектор с мухой|Детектор с мухой]]

> Методы ночной детекции с обучаемым или инвариантным к освещению фронт-эндом (YOLA, FeatEnHancer, PE-YOLO, IA-YOLO, DENet), их приросты против сильной базы и на ночи BDD100K; сопоставление с нашей сетчаткой-ламиной (адаптация фоторецептора и центр-окружение) как фронт-эндом детектора; что честно сравнивать.

## Суть
Low-light detectors put a small trainable or illumination-invariant module in front of (or inside the stem of) a standard detector and train it end-to-end with the detection loss, instead of enhancing images for human viewing. IA-YOLO predicts parameters of differentiable image filters (gamma, contrast, white balance, sharpening); DENet and PE-YOLO split the image into a Laplacian pyramid and enhance each band; FeatEnHancer fuses multi-scale enhanced features with attention; YOLA computes illumination-invariant features from neighbouring-pixel and neighbouring-channel relations under a Lambertian model using learned zero-mean kernels (essentially learned centre-surround operators on log intensity); FRBNet (NeurIPS 2025) does the same in the frequency domain. Reported gains are 1-3 mAP50 on ExDark/DARK FACE against old baselines (YOLOv3, TOOD, RetinaNet). The project's retina-lamina (Naka-Rushton photoreceptor with local temporal adaptation plus lamina centre-surround contrast) belongs to the same family, with one property none of them has: it adapts over time using the video history.

## Место мухи
Role: front-end of the detector (input channel or stem adapter), using only the retina-lamina part of the fly pipeline, not the medulla/T4/T5 network. Concretely: compute per raw 30 Hz frame the photoreceptor stage (Naka-Rushton I/(I + I_adapt + dark_floor) with I_adapt a spatially blurred (sigma 24 px) temporal running mean, tau 0.3 s, as in tracking/retina.py) and the lamina stage (subtract local mean, divide by local range, sigma 12 px), and feed the result to YOLO26s as an extra input channel (zero-initialised weights in the first convolution, so training starts exactly at the baseline) or as a replacement luminance channel. Why it is meaningful: the project's deficit at night is not blindness (4.5% day and night) but under-estimated confidence of real candidates (threshold losses 21.0% vs 15.5%), i.e. features are present but weak and badly normalised; local adaptation plus centre-surround is the classic biological solution to a 3-4 decade luminance range, and the project already measured that the biological form of the input matters (global frame normalisation 'norm' gives nothing, photoreceptor and lamina raise the fly-flow/error correlation 1.5x and 2.3x). It is also the mechanistic cousin of YOLA's zero-mean kernels, so YOLA is the natural strong comparator. What the fly adds beyond YOLA: temporal adaptation state carried across frames (YOLA, FeatEnHancer, PE-YOLO are still-image methods), which can only be exploited because BDD100K MOT provides 30 Hz video around each 5 Hz labelled frame. Caveat: the retina gain so far is fly-specific (DIS optical flow gets +0.0012, CI through zero, from the same retina), so a benefit for a learned CNN detector is not implied. The medulla (T4/T5) is not used here; motion evidence is the subject of D7.

## Какую потерю закрывает
THRESHOLD at night (21.0% vs 15.5% day; confidence under-estimated by +0.098 in the working band 0.1-0.5): a better-normalised input should raise the logits of real candidates that the detector already localises, which is what the low-light literature reports as recall gains (YOLA YOLOv3 recall 84.6 -> 86.1, TOOD 91.9 -> 93.8 on ExDark). BIRTH (age 0: 31% night vs 68% at 10+, two thirds through threshold): indirectly, because first appearances are small/far and low contrast; but temporal adaptation is history-dependent and does not prefer new objects. OVEREXPOSURE: Naka-Rushton compression with local adaptation also compresses headlight glare and bright sky; YOLA's COCO 'over-light' test (TOOD 40.5 -> 41.2 mAP) is the only literature hint. OCCLUSION at night (5.4x penalty): not directly. SMALL objects (56.7% found): partially; lamina centre-surround at sigma 12 px at 960 input may help mid-size objects more than tiny ones. BLINDNESS (4.5%): not targeted, consistent with the project's closure of frequency/WTConv work aimed at blindness.

## Пересечение с чужими работами
Heavy overlap. Learned illumination-invariant or enhancement front-ends trained jointly with the detector: IA-YOLO 2022, DENet 2022, MAET 2021, PE-YOLO 2023, FeatEnHancer 2023, YOLA 2024 (zero-mean centre-surround-like kernels, 0.008M parameters), FRBNet 2025, DAI-Net 2024 (Retinex reflectance). Bio-inspired dark adaptation for night detection: Hung & Lin 2024 (pupil and photoreceptor simulation, gamma correction, claimed +1.5 to +6% mAP) [uncertain: full text not read]. Photoreceptor adaptation as a trainable CNN layer: Idrees et al. 2024 (retina response prediction). Photoreceptor + LMC front-end before insect motion detectors: Brinkworth & O'Carroll 2009, Wiederman et al. 2008. No paper found that uses the FlyVis/connectome photoreceptor-lamina stage with temporal adaptation as a detector input for driving video [uncertain: limited search]. cannot_claim: 'bio-inspired front-end improves night detection' in general; illumination invariance via local ratios/centre-surround; joint training of a front-end with the detection loss; any comparison only against plain RGB. A claim is possible only if the temporal-adaptation retina-lamina channel beats both a YOLA-IIM arm and a same-input non-temporal arm at equal training budget across seeds.

## Результаты у авторов
ExDark (7363 images, 12 classes, mAP50, 608x608, 24 epochs, COCO-pretrained), as tabulated by YOLA: YOLOv3 baseline 71.0 -> DENet 71.3, MAET 72.5, IAT 72.6, YOLA 72.7 (+1.7); TOOD baseline 72.5 -> DENet 73.5, MAET 74.3, YOLA 75.2 (+2.7); enhancement-first methods KinD/SMG/NeRCo lower the baseline (68.5-69.4 with YOLOv3). FRBNet (NeurIPS 2025, same protocol): YOLOv3 74.9 (+3.9), TOOD 75.4 (+2.9); FRBNet's reproduction of FeatEnHancer gives 71.2 (YOLOv3) and 74.6 (TOOD). UG2+DARK FACE (YOLA table): YOLOv3 60.0 -> YOLA 61.5; TOOD 62.1 -> YOLA 67.4; FRBNet table uses a different split (YOLOv3 baseline 54.8 -> 57.7, TOOD 57.0 -> 65.1). YOLA ablation (TOOD): IIM alone 72.5 -> 73.8, with zero-mean constraint 74.7, full 75.2; COCO well-lit TOOD 41.7 -> 42.3 mAP, over-lit 40.5 -> 41.2. FeatEnHancer (own paper): ExDark RetinaNet 72.1 -> 72.6 mAP50 (+0.5) but Featurized Query R-CNN 74.5 -> 86.3 (+11.8); DARK FACE RetinaNet 47.3 -> 47.2, FQ R-CNN 67.5 -> 69.0; ACDC night segmentation 45.7 -> 54.9 mIoU; DarkVision video detection (SELSA) +1.8 mAP. PE-YOLO (ExDark, YOLOv3, 30 epochs): 76.4 -> 78.0 mAP50 at 53.6 vs 54.0 FPS; per class car 83.0 -> 83.9, people 81.5 -> 81.8, bicycle 79.8 -> 84.7; in the same table MAET 77.7, IAT-YOLO 77.8, DENet 77.3. IA-YOLO (YOLOv3, hybrid normal+synthetic-dark training): VOC_Dark test 52.28 -> 59.40, ExDark 37.03 -> 40.37 vs hybrid-data YOLOv3; vs YOLOv3 trained on normal VOC only 36.42 -> 40.37. DAI-Net (zero-shot day->night, DSFD face detector): released checkpoints 28.0 mAP zero-shot vs 52.9 fully supervised on DARK FACE. WTEFNet (preprint 2025): BDD100K night mAP50 49.0 with YOLOv12 vs 2PCNet 46.4 and UIA+YOLOv12 46.1; the plain YOLOv12 baseline is not reported. All of these use still images; camera motion and frame rate are irrelevant to the methods; no video-temporal front-end appears in the detection results.

## Проверка против сильной базы
Weak. (1) Baselines are YOLOv3 (2018), TOOD (2021), RetinaNet and Faster R-CNN; none uses a YOLO11/YOLO26-class detector trained at 960 with modern augmentation (HSV/brightness jitter, mosaic), which already provides part of the illumination robustness. (2) Gains depend strongly on the detector: FeatEnHancer +0.5 on RetinaNet vs +11.8 on FQ R-CNN; reproductions by others are smaller than self-reported (FeatEnHancer on YOLOv3 ExDark: 71.2 vs baseline 71.0 in YOLA/FRBNet tables). (3) Baseline numbers for the same detector on the same dataset differ between papers (ExDark YOLOv3 71.0 in YOLA vs 76.4 in PE-YOLO; DARK FACE YOLOv3 60.0 vs 54.8), so cross-paper deltas are not comparable. (4) No paper reports seeds or confidence intervals; gains of 1-3 mAP50 are of the same order as the project's 12-epoch seed spread (0.0144 mAP) and far above the night-mAP50 control SD (0.0031) only if they hold. (5) No equal-information control: the front-end arm usually also gets extra parameters/channels; the right control is the same channel count with a non-biological transform (global normalisation, gamma, or a raw gray duplicate). (6) No independent reproduction on BDD100K night found for YOLA/FeatEnHancer/PE-YOLO/IA-YOLO [uncertain]; the BDD100K night numbers that exist (WTEFNet, 2PCNet) lack a plain modern baseline.

## Разрез по условиям
ExDark has 12 lighting sub-types (low, ambient, object, single, weak, strong, screen, window, shadow, twilight) but the papers above report aggregate mAP only (per-class tables in PE-YOLO). YOLA adds a synthetic over-light COCO split. DARK FACE is a small-object (tiny faces) night set, the only proxy for small objects. No occlusion, first-appearance or day/night split on BDD100K for these methods; WTEFNet reports BDD100K night, SHIFT night, nuScenes night and rain/no-rain without plain baselines [uncertain].

## Дешёвая проверка
Gate F0, frozen detector, CPU/GPU about 2-3 hours, no training: test whether the retina-lamina channel carries detection evidence that YOLO26s discards. On runs/det_cache_ctl_960 (200 val videos, conf 0.01) take night candidates in the band [0.01, 0.25) and day candidates as a check; for the 7 local val videos with raw 30 Hz video (data/bdd100k/videos/val, runs/video_rate) compute the retina-lamina output with the full temporal history; per candidate compute inside-box vs ring statistics (mean adapted contrast, contrast energy, edge density) under four input transforms with identical feature definitions: raw gray, global normalisation ('norm' mode), YOLA-style fixed zero-mean log-DoG (no temporal state), photoreceptor+lamina with temporal adaptation. Fit logistic heads conf + features on train-split videos (the 1400 train videos are local) and evaluate on held-out videos with video bootstrap, exactly like q_gate1. Pre-registered criterion: the temporal retina-lamina head must beat the conf-only head and the raw-gray head on night band candidates by more than the split-based plank (q_gate1 plank was 0.0081 AUC) and beat the non-temporal log-DoG head; failure means the front-end is unlikely to help through the threshold mechanism. Caution: the q_gate1 quality map and class/geometry/scene correction (AUC 0.9025 -> 0.9005) failed under this protocol, so F0 is a necessary, not sufficient, condition. Gate F1, short training, about 4-6 GPU hours: fine-tune from weights/baseline_seed42/best.pt for 2 epochs at 640 on the MOT train labels, 3 seeds per arm, arms: (A) RGB + duplicate gray channel (channel-matched control), (B) RGB + temporal retina-lamina channel (precomputed from the 30 Hz train videos), optionally (C) RGB + YOLA IIM (learned, 0.008M params). Zero-initialise the new first-conv weights; log gradient norm of the new channel weights every N steps (DRB lesson: a branch without gradient). Criterion: night mAP50 (960 eval) gain of B over A >= +0.010 (about 3x the control SD 0.0031) with all 3 paired seeds positive, day mAP50 not worse than -0.003, and B > C if C is run; otherwise stop before any 12- or 100-epoch run.

## Стоимость у нас
F0: retina-lamina on 7 val videos at 30 Hz (~8.4k frames) is minutes with blur_downscale=4 (about 16x faster smoothing), plus feature extraction and logistic heads on CPU, about 1-2 h total; computing it for train-split videos used to fit heads adds about 1 h [uncertain]. F1: preprocessing the extra channel for all labelled train frames requires running the retina over the preceding raw frames of each of the 1400 local train videos (stateful, about 1.7M raw frames; at a few ms per frame with downscaled blurs, several hours of CPU, can run at Idle priority) [uncertain]; 2-epoch fine-tunes at 640 cost about 0.7 h each (12 epochs = 4.3 h), 6-9 runs = about 4-6.5 h. Full confirmation: 12 epochs x 3 seeds x 2-3 arms = 26-39 h; 100-epoch confirmation about 35 h per run. Ultralytics needs a 4-channel input (custom channel count in the dataset/model config and first-conv weight surgery) [uncertain: exact support in the installed Ultralytics version]. Inference cost: retina-lamina per frame adds a few ms on CPU or less on GPU; YOLA IIM adds 0.008M parameters.

## Ожидаемый эффект
Night mAP50 change for YOLO26s: likely 0 to +0.01 (control 0.3772 +/- 0.0031), smaller than the literature's +1.7 to +3.9 because those gains are against YOLOv3/TOOD/RetinaNet without strong brightness augmentation and without seeds [uncertain]. Threshold-step share at night: a successful front-end would lower 21.0% by at most 1-2 pp (the recall gains reported by YOLA on ExDark are +1.5 to +1.9 points) [uncertain]. Tracker DetA/FN: proportionally small, roughly +0.2 to +0.5 DetA if night mAP50 rises by +0.01 [uncertain]. The temporal adaptation of the retina is the only element with a plausible edge over still-image front-ends, but there is no published evidence for its benefit to a CNN detector; the fly-specific nature of the retina gain measured so far argues for a modest prior.

## Риски
(1) Seed noise: 12-epoch seed spread 0.0144 mAP overall, night mAP50 control SD 0.0031; a 2-epoch fine-tune may show a sign that flips at 100 epochs (as DRB did), so F1 is only a kill test, not a proof. (2) Branch without gradient: an extra channel can be ignored by the network; zero-init plus gradient logging and an ablation at inference (zero the channel) must show the network uses it. (3) Compute: preprocessing stateful retina over 1.7M raw frames and a 4-channel data pipeline; +17% time was a cost already criticised for DRB; the retina must be cheap at inference. (4) Augmentation interaction: Ultralytics HSV/brightness augmentation applied to RGB but not to the precomputed retina channel breaks consistency; either disable brightness augmentation in both arms or compute the retina online after augmentation (but then without temporal history). (5) Train/test mismatch: temporal adaptation needs preceding frames; at inference on a video this is natural, but for still-image evaluation (BDD100K detection set) it is undefined. (6) Non-comparable baselines across papers (ExDark YOLOv3 71.0 vs 76.4); compare only inside the project. (7) Equal-information: an extra channel adds capacity; the duplicate-gray control is mandatory. (8) Licences of YOLA/FRBNet/PE-YOLO code not checked; reimplementing YOLA IIM is small (a few zero-mean convolutions). (9) Overexposure: Naka-Rushton with a dark_floor may amplify compression noise in black regions (the reason the floor was added).

## Как подписать заимствование
Borrowed: the idea of a task-trained illumination front-end (IA-YOLO, DENet, PE-YOLO, FeatEnHancer, YOLA, FRBNet), the Lambertian zero-mean invariant-kernel formulation (YOLA, as a comparator arm), the photoreceptor + LMC model idea (Brinkworth & O'Carroll 2009), FlyVis retina/lamina cell types (Lappalainen et al. 2024). Own: the simplified retina-lamina implementation (tracking/retina.py), the temporal-adaptation input channel computed from 30 Hz video around 5 Hz labels, the diagnosis that the night deficit is the threshold step (not blindness) and therefore the frozen-detector candidate-evidence gate F0, the channel-matched and non-temporal controls, and the zero-init/gradient-logging protocol derived from the DRB failure. Any gain must be attributed to the temporal adaptation only if it beats the non-temporal and YOLA arms.
