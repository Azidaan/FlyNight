# D14 Обучение под заниженную уверенность: взвешивание трудных и новорождённых объектов, функции потерь качества

↑ [[07 Исследования/Детектор с мухой/Детектор с мухой|Детектор с мухой]]

> Почему детектор занижает уверенность на ночных и только что появившихся объектах и как это лечат обучением (Varifocal, quality focal, PISA, взвешивание по трудности, калибровка детекторов 2023–2026); связь с нашим рельсом «взвешивание по возрасту объекта»; может ли муха дать вес (временное свидетельство) — взвешивание по мухе.

## Суть
Modern one-stage detectors, YOLO26 included, do not train the classification score to mean 'object present'. They train it to mean 'object present AND the box is good': the target score is an IoU-aware quality (Varifocal Loss, Quality Focal Loss, task-aligned assignment). The local Ultralytics 8.4.84 code shows this directly: in utils/tal.py the BCE target for a ground-truth object is its alignment metric rescaled so that the best anchor's target equals that object's maximum predicted-box IoU. So any object whose predicted boxes are poor at that moment (night blur, low contrast, partial occlusion, first appearance with a truncated or ambiguous extent) is trained toward a LOW score. The measured night/age-0 underconfidence is therefore partly built into the objective, not only a data shortage. There are three known ways to fix this: (1) re-weight samples (PISA, focal/hard-example weighting, ProgLoss/STAL in YOLO26, or the project's age weighting); (2) change the quality target (MAL in DEIM, a target floor, or decoupling objectness from localization quality); (3) recalibrate after training (Platt or isotonic, size- and position-conditioned calibration), but a monotone recalibration cannot change ranking or AUC. The fly can contribute temporal visual evidence, as a weight or as a target modifier, that a single-frame detector does not have.

## Место мухи
Three roles, from the strongest to the weakest justification. (1) Fly evidence as a gate on the age weight, the recommended role. The age rail upweights every young object, including objects that are effectively invisible in that frame (dark, glare-washed, almost fully occluded). Upweighting those teaches the detector to fire on darkness and raises FP. A per-object fly visibility score separates visible-but-underconfident objects, which should be upweighted, from invisible ones, which should be kept at weight 1 or downweighted as label-noise-like. The score is retina-lamina contrast energy inside the GT box relative to an annulus, plus T4/T5 opponent motion energy relative to ego-flow. The weight becomes w = 1 + alpha * PROFILE[age_bin] * g(fly_evidence), delivered through the existing class_weights hook (birth_weight.py), so no new plumbing is needed. Lamina and T4/T5 are the right cell types: lamina L1/L2 carry light-adapted contrast, which is the night problem, and T4/T5 carry local motion, which is the evidence available at age 0. (2) Fly evidence as a quality-target modifier. TAL makes the target equal to the object's best predicted IoU, so objects with poor boxes get low targets. For objects where the fly says 'visible and moving coherently', the classification target can be floored at tau (for example 0.5) while the box loss is left unchanged. This decouples 'is there an object' from 'how good is my box' only where there is independent evidence. (3) Fly as an online temporal teacher for score consistency: fly evidence accumulated over the last k frames raises the score of a candidate (inference-side). This overlaps with D13 rescoring and with COVTrack++-style temporal confidence adjustment. Weighting by the fly cannot give the single-frame detector temporal information. It can only redirect its capacity, which is why role (1) is framed as a selection of which hard examples are learnable, not as feeding motion into the detector.

## Какую потерю закрывает
Primarily THRESHOLD: real candidates are underconfident (21.0% of night objects lost at the threshold vs 15.5% by day; night score deficit +0.098). It also targets BIRTH, since 2/3 of the 31% vs 68% age-0 gap goes through the threshold. Rationale: (a) IoU-scaled targets mechanically lower the scores of objects with worse boxes, and night and newborn objects plausibly have worse boxes (to be measured, see cheap_gate); (b) the project measured that augmentation removes young objects disproportionately (survival of age-0 objects 0.787 under full augmentation vs 1.000 with geometry off, about four fifths of it from mosaic), so they are under-represented in the loss; (c) post-hoc rescoring by class, geometry and scene did not raise AUC (0.9025 -> 0.9005), and monotone calibration (Platt or isotonic) cannot raise AUC at all, so the remaining lever is training-time. Occlusion at night (5.4x penalty) is addressed if the visibility gate keeps partially visible occluded objects in the upweighted set. BLINDNESS (4.5%) is not addressed: weighting can only reorder candidates that the head already forms, apart from second-order effects of STAL-like assignment on tiny objects. Small objects (56.7% found) are only addressed through assignment (STAL is already in YOLO26), not through weighting.

## Пересечение с чужими работами
Hard or prime sample weighting (focal loss, OHEM, GHM, PISA), IoU-aware score targets (IoU-Net, GFL/QFL, VFL, TOOD), matchability-aware targets (DEIM MAL), progressive loss re-weighting and small-target assignment (YOLO26 ProgLoss/STAL) and detector calibration (Kuppers 2020; Munir et al. CVPR 2022/2023 train-time calibration; Cal-DETR NeurIPS 2023; Kuzucu ECCV 2024) are all established. Night-specific training exists: 2PCNet uses NightAug and small-scale pseudo-labels and reaches 46.4 vs 41.1 AP on BDD100K night. Temporal score recovery exists on the tracker side (ByteTrack low-score association; COVTrack++ confidence adjustment). Not found: weighting detector training by the TEMPORAL ROLE of the object in its trajectory (age since first appearance), or by evidence from a biological motion and contrast model. cannot_claim: 'first to address detector underconfidence', 'first IoU-target fix', 'first calibration by object size or position' (Kuppers 2020 conditions on position and scale). Claimable if results support it: an age-of-object weight derived from the tracker-side cost of a miss, gated by fly visibility evidence, evaluated at equal birth precision.

## Результаты у авторов
VFNet (COCO, FCOS+ATSS base): about +2.0 AP over the baseline across backbones. The full VFNet adds a star-shaped box representation and box refinement; the share due to Varifocal Loss alone is smaller [uncertain: about +1 AP]. Best model 55.1 AP test-dev. GFL (COCO test-dev, ResNet-101): 45.0 AP vs ATSS 43.6 (+1.4, QFL+DFL together); best 48.2 AP. PISA (COCO): about +2 AP over random sampling and over OHEM and focal loss, for one- and two-stage detectors including ResNeXt-101. Baselines are 2019-2021 detectors. YOLO26 (COCO, own ablation from a YOLO11s 46.6 AP base): DFL removal +0.2 AP, STAL +0.2 AP mainly on small objects, ProgLoss +0.3 AP for end-to-end variants, MuSGD 47.4 at 500 epochs vs 47.0 at 600 with SGD. This is the strongest base, and the effects are a few tenths of AP. DEIM (COCO): DEIM-D-FINE-L 54.7 AP, X 56.5 AP, with about 50% shorter training than D-FINE. MAL's isolated contribution is part of the ablation [uncertain: about +0.3-0.6 AP]. Calibration (Kuzucu ECCV 2024, COCO): post-hoc isotonic regression on D-DETR beats the train-time Cal-DETR by more than 7 D-ECE, so post-hoc calibration is a strong baseline for calibration error, though it leaves ranking and AP unchanged. 2PCNet (BDD100K day->night, Faster R-CNN R50): AP 41.1 source-only -> 46.4, vs 46.2 for the oracle trained on labeled night. Per-class: traffic light 41.0 -> 49.4, pedestrian 50.0 -> 54.4, rider 28.9 -> 30.8. This is a domain-adaptation setting (no night labels), unlike ours (night labels present). Frame rate and camera motion are not modeled by any of these except 2PCNet's BDD100K images from a moving car, used as still images.

## Проверка против сильной базы
The loss-function gains were measured against the detectors of their time: VFL/GFL/PISA against RetinaNet, FCOS and ATSS. Their ideas are now inside the strong base: Ultralytics YOLO uses TOOD's task-aligned assigner with IoU-scaled BCE targets, and YOLO26 adds STAL and ProgLoss. On that base the published increments are +0.2 to +0.3 AP. So the 'free' gains from quality losses are already spent, and a new weighting must win on the specific subsets (night, age 0) rather than overall. Independent reproduction: VFL and GFL were reproduced widely (MMDetection, PaddleDetection, Ultralytics adoption). PISA saw less uptake. Equal-budget checks: DEIM explicitly compares at shorter schedules. The Kuzucu ECCV 2024 paper is itself a strong-baseline check, showing that earlier train-time calibration claims weaken against properly tuned post-hoc calibrators. The project protocol already requires the right control for age weighting: comparison at equal birth precision (87.64%), not at an equal threshold. Without it, any weighting that shifts the operating point looks like a gain.

## Разрез по условиям
COCO papers report APs, APm and APl only. No night, glare, occlusion or first-appearance breakdown. 2PCNet: BDD100K night with per-class AP. Small classes such as traffic light and sign gain the most from small-scale pseudo-labels. Calibration papers: Kuppers 2020 reports calibration as a function of box position and scale. No paper reports results by object age in a trajectory; the project's [age_first_appearance↗](obsidian://open?vault=ObsidianFlyVis&file=02%20%D0%AD%D0%BA%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D1%8B%2Fage_first_appearance) card is the only such breakdown here (age 0: 31.34% found at night on the 640 stand vs 67.89% at age 10+, 30.41-point gap at 960, 66% through the threshold).

## Дешёвая проверка
All gates run on existing caches (conf-0.01 detections at 960 on val and train videos, GT with track IDs, foveal fly flow with retina-lamina). No training. Gate D14-A, IoU mechanism (about 1 h): for each GT object, take the best-IoU candidate at conf >= 0.01 and split into found (above threshold), threshold-lost and blind. Compare the IoU distribution of threshold-lost vs found, night vs day, age 0 vs 10+. Pre-registered prediction: median best-candidate IoU of threshold-lost objects is lower than that of found ones by >= 0.05, and night age-0 objects have lower IoU than day age-10+. If true, the low score is partly the trained IoU-scaled target, and a target floor (fly role 2) is justified. If the IoUs are equal, underconfidence is not caused by the quality target, and only weighting remains. Gate D14-B, visibility separability (2-3 h): compute fly evidence (lamina contrast energy and T4/T5 motion contrast in box vs annulus) for GT objects at age 0-2 on train-cache videos. Test whether, among threshold-lost young objects, there is a sizable visible group: fly evidence above the 50th percentile of found objects. Pre-registered pass: >= 30% of threshold-lost young night objects are fly-visible, and fly evidence predicts found vs threshold-lost with AUC >= 0.60 after controlling for area, class and brightness (logistic, CI excludes 0.5). Also check the shuffled-connectome and receptor-only controls. If the fly is no better than receptor-only, use the cheaper receptor contrast as the gate and attribute nothing to the connectome. Gate D14-C, weighting dry run (about 1 h): with birth_weight.py at the chosen alpha, log the effective weight mass per category over one epoch (age 0-2 night, visible vs invisible). This confirms that the fly gate actually changes which objects receive the extra weight (overlap of upweighted sets < 0.8 Jaccard vs plain age weighting); otherwise the two arms are the same experiment.

## Стоимость у нас
Gates A-C: hours on existing caches. Gate B needs fly evidence on GT boxes of young objects in train videos; foveal eyes per object over a few frames, the same pipeline as the per-track eyes (about 214 s per video including detection and DIS in the cache build) [uncertain: 2-4 h for 130 videos restricted to young objects]. Full test: fine-tune from best.pt is the cheapest valid design (for example 3 epochs, about 1.1 h per run at 21.5 min/epoch). Arms: control (alpha=0), age weighting, age x fly-visibility weighting, and optionally the target-floor variant. 3 seeds each: 4 x 3 x 1.1 h, about 13 h. 12-epoch runs from the standard init: 4 x 3 x 4.3 h, about 52 h. 100-epoch confirmation of the winner vs control: 2 x 35 h. Fly evidence for all training objects must be precomputed once and stored in the label channel (the fractional-class trick in make_birth_labels.py can carry one more quantized feature) [uncertain: tens of hours of fly inference for 554 393 objects unless restricted to young objects]. Inference cost: zero, since weighting acts only in training.

## Ожидаемый эффект
Literature prior on a base that already has TAL, STAL and ProgLoss: overall mAP change within about +/-0.3, which is below the 0.0144 seed spread at 12 epochs. So the effect must be read on subsets. Target subset: age-0 found rate at equal birth precision. Plain age weighting can at most close the threshold part of the gap (about 66% of 30.41 points, about 20 points). A realistic outcome for loss reweighting is a fraction of that [uncertain: +2 to +6 points of age-0 recall at night], with a small FP cost. The fly gate is expected to improve the FP side, not the recall side. Tracker level: birth-related FN are a subset of the 15 763 FN, and DetA was flat under all tracker-side changes (58.21 -> 58.17). A detector-side gain of a few points in young-object recall would be the first lever to move DetA [uncertain: +0.2-0.6 DetA]. The status of the age rail must be stated honestly. The [birth_weighting↗](obsidian://open?vault=ObsidianFlyVis&file=09%20%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BF%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%BE%2Fbirth_weighting) card is 'running', only the alpha=0 run of the sweep has a results.csv (runs/birth_alpha/a0), and the alpha=0.25 run has weights but no results file. No result of age weighting exists yet. The fly-gated variant should be run only after plain age weighting shows a signal on age-0 recall.

## Риски
1) Operating-point illusion: any loss that raises scores globally increases recall at a fixed threshold. Compare at equal birth precision or equal FP per frame, as the [birth_weighting↗](obsidian://open?vault=ObsidianFlyVis&file=09%20%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BF%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%BE%2Fbirth_weighting) acceptance criteria already require. 2) Seed noise 0.0144 mAP at 12 epochs and sign flip at 100 epochs (DRB): report subset metrics with paired seeds and bootstrap CI, and confirm at 100 epochs. 3) Weight with no effect: E2ELoss decays the one-to-many weight from 0.8 to 0.1. The patch acts on both branches, but the inference branch's effective weight changes over training; log the weighted-loss share of the target subset per epoch. Weights that are normalized away by target_scores_sum can silently cancel; check the gradient magnitude on upweighted anchors. 4) Label noise: BDD100K MOT labels at 5 Hz include objects that are invisible in the frame. Upweighting them trains hallucination, which is exactly what the fly gate is meant to prevent; if the gate is weak, FP will rise. 5) Augmentation confound: mosaic removes young objects (survival 0.787). Weighting interacts with augmentation, so keep augmentation identical across arms or treat it as a separate single factor. 6) Target-floor variant: flooring the cls target decouples score from IoU and may hurt AP at high IoU (AP75). Report AP50 and AP50-95 separately. 7) Calibration confusion: post-hoc calibration improves ECE but not AUC or AP; do not report ECE gains as detection gains. 8) Licenses: Ultralytics AGPL-3.0, so modified loss code that is distributed inherits AGPL; DEIM/D-FINE Apache-2.0; BDD100K non-commercial.
